Block Reels. Keep Instagram.

There is no switch in Instagram to turn Reels off. You can still stop them on Android, after a time limit or a number of videos, while your DMs, Stories and posts carry on as normal.

Why you can't just turn Reels off

Instagram does not give you a setting that removes Reels. The Reels tab sits in the bottom bar, Reels appear in the home feed, and they show up in Explore. The time tools Instagram does offer apply to the whole app and are reminders you can dismiss, so they do not help if the app is fine and the problem is the feed of short videos.

That leaves you with an outside tool. On Android, an app with accessibility access can tell when the Reels player is on screen and step in, which is exactly what the reducers below do.

Pick how the limit is measured

Curbox's Short-Form Video Content reducer has two modes. Choose the one that matches how you think about your habit.

Reel Count Based

Best if you lose track of "how many"

Curbox counts the videos you watch and blocks Reels at your number. Reels you skip past in a fast swipe are not counted, so the number reflects videos you actually stopped on. A count is concrete: "20 Reels" is easier to feel than "15 minutes".

Time Based

Best if you think in minutes

Curbox tracks the time you spend watching short-form video each day and blocks when you hit the limit. Useful if you watch fewer but longer Reels, or if you want this to match a screen-time goal.

Both modes reset each day. Want Reels gone entirely? Set a very low limit and pair it with a warning screen of Never Unlock.

Set it up in two minutes

  1. Install Curbox and finish onboarding. It asks for display over other apps, notifications, and the App Blocker accessibility service.
  2. Open Reducers → Short-Form Video Content.
  3. Turn on the main switch in the top right.
  4. Choose Reel Count Based or Time Based and set your limit.
  5. Optional: tap Configure Allowed Schedule to choose the days and hours the limit applies. A single range cannot cross midnight, so split overnight ranges in two.
  6. Optional: tap Configure Warning Screen to decide what happens at the limit: no way through, a wait, or a task like scanning a QR code in another room.
  7. Optional: allow Reels opened from your DM inbox.

The full reference is in the Short-Form Video docs, and every pause-screen option is in the unlock challenges guide.

One limit across every short-video app

The count and the time are combined across all supported apps: Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels. With a limit of 20 videos:

This matters because the habit is usually "short videos", not "Instagram". Blocking one app tends to move the scrolling to the next one. Community-added support also covers some modded clients, though the Curbox project does not endorse mods.

See it before you block it

If you are not ready for a hard limit, start with awareness. Two nudges help:

  • Short-Form Video Counter (Reducers → Short-Form Video Counter) puts a live number on screen as you scroll, and shows a total for today plus a weekly chart.
  • Attention span tracking measures how long you actually watch each short video and shows your average attention span in your usage stats. Watching that number fall over a long scroll is a useful wake-up call.

A Reels Widget for your home screen shows today's count without opening anything. Many people find a visible number enough to change their habit, and add a block later only if they need it.

Two more gaps to close

Reels in the browser. Once the app is limited, instagram.com is the obvious way around. In Reducers → Keywords And Websites, add instagram.com/reels to block only Reels pages, or /reels to block that path on every site. It works in Chrome, Brave, Firefox and other supported browsers. See blocking websites on Android for the full syntax.

Reels in the home feed. F-Droid / GitHub build UI Hider can hide Instagram's home feed except the Following tab, and hide the Explore tab, so fewer Reels are put in front of you in the first place. Turn it on in Reducers → UI Hider. It is experimental and not part of the Playstore build. More in the UI Hider docs.

Questions

Can you turn off Reels in Instagram settings?

No. Instagram does not offer a setting that removes or disables Reels. Its own time tools apply to the whole app. To block only Reels you need a separate app such as Curbox.

Will blocking Reels also block Instagram DMs?

No. Curbox's Short-Form Video reducer only stops the Reels player. Messages, Stories, posts and your profile keep working. You can also allow Reels that are opened from your DM inbox.

Does a fast swipe count as a watched Reel?

No. Reels you skip past in a fast swipe are not counted toward a count-based limit.

Can I block YouTube Shorts with the same limit?

Yes. The limit is combined across Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels, so 10 Reels and 10 Shorts count as 20 videos.

How do I block Reels on instagram.com in a browser?

Add instagram.com/reels as a keyword in Curbox's Keywords And Websites reducer. It works in supported browsers such as Chrome, Brave and Firefox.

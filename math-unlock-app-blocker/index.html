Feature · Adaptive math unlock

Solve math to unlock apps.

Curbox is a free Android app blocker that can make you solve math to unlock apps. Open Instagram during a block and you get a few problems first. You choose how hard they are, from simple sums to fractions and roots, and every wrong answer adds a wait.

How does math unlock work in an app blocker?

When you open an app that Curbox is blocking, you see a pause screen instead of the app. If the unlock challenge is set to Adaptive Math, that screen shows a problem. Answer it and the next one appears. Once you reach the number of correct answers you chose, the proceed button shows up and you can continue.

A wrong answer does not just let you try again. It adds a wait first. That one rule matters more than the difficulty, because it stops you from mashing in guesses until one sticks. The quickest way through is to actually think, which is the whole point: by the time you have worked out a fraction, you are thinking, not scrolling.

What do the levels look like, from 1 to 10?

You pick a starting level. The docs describe the two ends of the range: level 1 is simple arithmetic, and level 10 combines decimals, fractions, roots, powers and order of operations. The three settings that decide how hard it is

Correct answersHow many problems you must get right. One is a speed bump; five is a commitment.
Starting levelFrom 1 to 10. Start lower than you think: a challenge you resent is one you will turn off.
Wrong-answer waitBuilt in. A wrong answer adds a wait before your next try, so guessing costs time.

You can also add a delay timer before the first problem appears, a custom message on the pause screen, and proceed limits that cap how many times you can get through in a window, however good you are at maths.

How to set up math unlock in Curbox

  1. Install Curbox from the downloads page and finish the short onboarding.
  2. Go to Reducers → Apps, tap +, name the group and tap Select Apps.
  3. Pick a blocking mode: Usage Based for a daily limit, Time Based for allowed hours, or On Each Open for every launch.
  4. Tap Configure Warning Screen. Set the Unlock Challenge to Require effort to unlock and choose Adaptive Math.
  5. Choose the number of correct answers and the starting level, then tap Done.

For Reels and Shorts, do the same from Reducers → Short-Form Video Content. All options are in the unlock challenges guide.

Why math works as an unlock challenge (and when it doesn't)

Scrolling and arithmetic use your attention in opposite ways. A feed asks for nothing: it pulls you along one swipe at a time. A math problem asks you to hold numbers in your head and work something out. Switching from one to the other is enough to jolt you out of autopilot, and once you are thinking clearly again, you can decide whether you really want the app.

Math is also a challenge you can do anywhere. A QR code in another room is stronger at home but useless on a train. Problems on the screen go wherever your phone goes.

The weakness is that you get better at it. If level 4 starts to feel automatic after a few weeks, raise the level or the number of answers. And if maths makes you anxious rather than thoughtful, pick a different challenge. Friction should feel like a pause, not a punishment.

Settings that work for different people

"I just open apps without noticing." Level 2, one correct answer, On Each Open. You will barely be slowed down, but you will notice every open, and noticing is half the battle.

"I keep going past my daily limit." Level 5, three correct answers, Usage Based. Past the limit, each extra session costs a minute of thought.

"Late-night scrolling is the problem." Level 8, three answers, Time Based outside your evening window. Fractions at midnight are a strong argument for sleep.

"I hate maths." Then this is the wrong challenge for you, and that is fine. Try scanning a QR code, an NFC tag, or typing a sentence instead. If the goal is to sit with the urge rather than solve it, look at urge surfing.

Math unlock questions

Is there an app that makes you solve math to unlock apps?

Yes. Curbox for Android has an Adaptive Math unlock challenge. When you open a blocked app, you have to answer a set number of math questions correctly before the proceed button appears.

How hard are the math problems?

You choose a starting level from 1 to 10. Level 1 is simple arithmetic. Level 10 combines decimals, fractions, roots, powers and order of operations. You also choose how many correct answers are required.

What happens if I get a math problem wrong?

A wrong answer adds a wait before you can try again. Guessing quickly does not get you in faster; it slows you down.

Can I just use a calculator?

You could, but by the time you have found the calculator and typed the problem in, you have already spent the pause the challenge was designed to create. The point is the interruption, not a test you must pass unaided.

Can I use math unlock for Reels and Shorts too?

Yes. The warning screen, including Adaptive Math, can be configured on App Pause groups and on the Short-Form Video Content reducer.

Feature · Anti-uninstall · Android

An app blocker that can't be uninstalled on impulse.

Every app blocker has the same weak spot: the uninstall button. If you want to prevent uninstalling your app blocker in a weak moment on Android, here is how Curbox layers its protection, what each layer really does, and how to switch it off when you decide to.

The goal is to beat the impulse, not the person. No app can stop the owner of a phone forever, and one that tried would be dangerous. A determined person can always find a way out: factory reset, safe mode, a computer. Good protection does something smaller and more useful. It turns a two-second tap into a slow, deliberate process, long enough for the urge to pass.

Four layers of protection against uninstalling

Think of it as rings around your rules. The outer rings are on by default in any setup. The inner ones are what make an app blocker hard to remove or bypass on impulse. Use as many as you need; most people need fewer than they think.

Layer 1Your rulesEvery variant

An App Pause group, a Reels limit or a Focus session. On its own, a rule stops the habit but not a change of heart: you can open Curbox and switch the rule off.

Layer 2A pause screen you cannot rushEvery variant

Choose Never Unlock to make a block final, or a demanding challenge such as a QR code kept in another room. Add Proceed Limits to cap how many times you can get through in a time window; once you hit the cap, the proceed button disappears until the window resets.

Layer 3Curbox blocks itselfEvery variant

You can add Curbox itself to an app blocking group. Then opening Curbox to loosen a rule hits the same pause screen as Instagram does. Pair it with a Time Based window so settings are only editable at a calm hour, such as Sunday morning.

Layer 4Anti-uninstall protectionF-Droid variant

Curbox uses Android's device administrator feature so it cannot simply be uninstalled while your blocks are active. Long-press, uninstall: nothing happens until device admin access is switched off in your phone's settings first. The Playstore variant does not include this layer and never asks for device admin access.

What device administrator access does, and does not do

"Device administrator" sounds alarming, so it is worth being exact. Curbox uses it for one job.

How to turn it off and uninstall

You should always know the way out before you lock the door. Here it is.

Removing Curbox when anti-uninstall is on

  1. Open your phone's Settings.
  2. Go to Security (or Security and privacy), then the section for device admin apps. The exact name and place vary by phone maker; searching Settings for "device admin" usually finds it.
  3. Select Curbox and deactivate it.
  4. Uninstall Curbox the normal way.

On some phones, deactivating device administrator access may require booting into safe mode first. Safe mode starts Android with only built-in apps running. How to enter it varies by model; on many phones you press and hold the power-off option in the power menu.

That process takes a few minutes and a little effort, which is the whole point. By the time you reach step three, you have had time to ask whether you really want to.

Questions

Is there an app blocker that can't be uninstalled?

Not in an absolute sense, and you would not want one. Android always lets the owner of a phone remove an app in the end. What an app blocker can do is make uninstalling slow and deliberate. Curbox's anti-uninstall protection uses Android's device administrator feature, so the app cannot be removed until that access is switched off in your phone's settings first.

How do I uninstall Curbox if anti-uninstall is on?

Deactivate Curbox's device administrator access in your phone's settings, usually under Security or Security and privacy, in a section called Device admin apps or similar. After that, Curbox uninstalls normally. On some phones you may need to restart into safe mode first to deactivate it.

Does device administrator access let Curbox lock or wipe my phone?

No. Curbox uses it only to resist being uninstalled. It does not lock your device, wipe anything, or collect any data. The Curbox Android app has no internet permission in any variant.

Which version of Curbox has anti-uninstall protection?

The F-Droid variant. The Playstore variant never asks for device administrator access. Both variants can add Curbox itself to an app blocking group and use Never Unlock and Proceed Limits. All official downloads are on the downloads page.

Can I stop myself changing my Curbox settings too?

Yes. Curbox can add itself to an App Pause group, so opening Curbox to loosen a rule hits the same pause screen as any other blocked app. Combine it with Never Unlock or a demanding unlock challenge for the hours you want to protect.

Do I need to turn anti-uninstall on again after restoring a backup?

Yes. Restoring a Curbox backup replaces your settings and rules, and you will need to turn uninstall protection on again afterwards.

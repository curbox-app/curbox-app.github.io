Feature · Usage tracking

A screen time tracker for Android that keeps your data on the phone.

Curbox is a free screen time tracker for Android. It shows how long you spend in each app and on each website, today and across the week, with no ads, no account, and no internet access. Your usage history is stored on your phone and nowhere else.

  1. 1Total today

    Your total screen time so far today, at the top of the tab. It is the one number worth glancing at a few times a day, and it is the same number the Screentime widget puts on your home screen.

  2. 2The weekly bar chart

    One bar per day for the current week. Tap a bar to see that day's total, and use the arrows to go back to earlier weeks. This is where patterns show up: the late night after a stressful day, or the weekend that quietly doubled.

    Usage tab → tap a day · arrows for past weeks

  3. 3Per-app list

    Every app you used, with today's time next to it. Background apps that inflate the numbers, like a launcher or a music player you never look at, can be hidden with the Ignore List.

    Usage tab → ⋮ → Ignore List

  4. 4Website time, per browser

    Most Android screen time trackers stop at "Chrome: 2 hours". Curbox also shows which websites you visited and for how long, grouped by browser. This works in Chrome, Brave, Firefox, Firefox F-Droid, Opera, Cromite, Vanadium, Helium, CalyxOS Chromium, the /e/OS Browser and Waterfox.

  5. 5Daily and weekly views, plus a heatmap

    Switch between daily and weekly usage from the app usage menu, or by zooming out. The advanced analytics view adds a heatmap that shows which hours of which days you reach for your phone most, which is often more useful than the total.

  6. 6A live session timer

    A small on-screen timer can show how long you have been in the current app. It is surprisingly effective, because "I'll just check" rarely feels like 25 minutes while it is happening.

An app usage tracker with no ads, and why that matters here

To measure screen time per app, a tracker needs to see which app is on screen. On Android, Curbox does that through a single Accessibility Service, the same one it uses to block apps and count short videos. That kind of access can see a lot, so it is worth asking where the data goes afterwards.

No internet permission

The Android app cannot connect to the internet in any variant. Your history physically cannot be uploaded.

No account

Install, grant the permissions, and the tracker starts. There is nothing to sign up for.

Open source

The code is public under GPL v3, so anyone can check what is recorded and where it is kept.

If you do want your numbers on more than one device, the separate, optional Curbox Sync companion app handles that. The tracker itself works fully offline. The privacy policy has the details.

Put your screen time on the home screen

From the three-dot menu on the Usage tab, Pin Widget adds one of two home-screen widgets:

  • Screentime Widget: your total screen time today.
  • Reels Widget: how many short-form videos you have watched today, counted across Instagram, YouTube and Facebook. The reels counter page explains how that count works.

This is the lowest-effort habit change in the whole app. Nothing is blocked. You just see the number every time you unlock your phone, and many people find that enough to start using it differently. If you want to go further later, the same data drives limits in App Pause.

Export your usage to CSV

Usage tab → ⋮ → Export saves your history as a CSV file you can open in any spreadsheet. You pick one of three shapes:

  • Daily Breakdown: one row per app per day. Good for trends over time.
  • Total Summary: one total row per app. Good for a quick ranking.
  • Both: the two together.

Things people actually do with the export

  1. A Sunday review. Open the Total Summary, sort by time, and look only at the top three apps. Decide on one change for the coming week.
  2. A pivot table by weekday. Load the Daily Breakdown into a spreadsheet and pivot by app and day of the week. Weekday and weekend habits are often completely different, and Curbox can give them different limits.
  3. Before and after. Export once before you set your first limit and again a month later. Comparing the two files is the most honest way to know if a change worked.
  4. Share it with someone you trust. A file you choose to send is very different from an app that reports on you in the background.

Set up the tracker in about five minutes

  1. Install Curbox from one of the official sources on the downloads page.
  2. Grant the three core permissions in onboarding: display over other apps, notifications, and the App Blocker accessibility service, which is also what measures your screen time. Shizuku can grant them all at once if you use it.
  3. Use your phone normally for a day. The Usage tab fills in as you go.
  4. Clean up the list. Add noisy background apps to the Ignore List so the totals reflect what you actually look at.
  5. Pin the Screentime Widget. Then leave it alone for a week before deciding on any limits.

The full reference is in the Usage Stats docs.

Screen time tracker FAQ

Doesn't Android already track screen time?

Yes. Digital Wellbeing shows per-app time and a daily total. Curbox adds website time for a long list of browsers, a heatmap of when you use your phone, short-video counts, CSV export, and limits that can act on the numbers.

Is there an app usage tracker with no ads?

Curbox has no ads, no analytics code and no internet permission. It is free and open source under GPL v3, so there is no business model that depends on your data.

Can I see which websites I spend time on?

Yes, in supported browsers: Chrome, Brave, Firefox, Firefox F-Droid, Opera, Cromite, Vanadium, Helium, CalyxOS Chromium, the /e/OS Browser and Waterfox. Website time is grouped by browser on the Usage tab.

Can I export my screen time data?

Yes. On the Usage tab, open the three-dot menu and choose Export. You get a CSV as a Daily Breakdown (one row per app per day), a Total Summary (one row per app), or both.

Does tracking screen time drain the battery?

Curbox is about a 3 MB download and uses roughly the RAM and battery of a keyboard app running in the background.

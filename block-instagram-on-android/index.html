How to block Instagram on Android.

"Block Instagram" means different things to different people. Some want it gone at bedtime, some want an hour a day, some only want Reels out of their life. Pick what you actually want to stop and jump straight to that recipe.

Before you start

The recipes below use Curbox, a free, open-source app blocker for Android. It is about a 3 MB download and setup takes a few minutes: install it, then grant the three permissions the onboarding asks for (display over other apps, notifications, and the App Blocker accessibility service). The accessibility service is how any Android blocker knows which app is on screen. Curbox has no internet permission, so what it sees stays on your phone.

Every recipe ends at the same place: a pause screen that appears when Instagram is opened outside your rules. What that screen lets you do is your choice, from "no way through" to "wait 30 seconds and think about it". That part is covered in the last section.

01

Block Instagram completely

  • App Pause
  • On Each Open
  • 2 minutes

Use this if you are taking a full break, or if Instagram is the app you open without thinking. On Each Open mode shows the pause screen every single time Instagram launches, regardless of the time or how much you have used it.

  1. Open Curbox and go to Reducers → Apps.
  2. Tap the + button in the bottom-right corner and name the group, for example "No Instagram".
  3. Tap Select Apps and pick Instagram. Add Threads or Facebook here too if they are part of the same habit.
  4. Choose the On Each Open blocking mode.
  5. Tap Configure Warning Screen and set the unlock challenge to Never Unlock if you want a real break.
  6. Tap Done. The block is active straight away.

Tip
Each group card has a toggle, so you can pause the block for a planned reason (posting for work, say) without deleting it and setting it up again.

02

Block Instagram only at certain hours

  • App Pause
  • Time Based
  • 3 minutes

Time Based mode flips the logic: you choose when Instagram is allowed, and it is blocked the rest of the time. For "no Instagram in bed", allow it from, say, 7 AM to 10 PM.

  1. Go to Reducers → Apps and tap +.
  2. Name it ("Instagram daytime only") and select Instagram.
  3. Choose Time Based.
  4. Open Configure Allowed Schedule and set the hours and days when Instagram may be used.
  5. Tap Done.

Watch out for midnight
A single time range in Curbox cannot cross midnight. If you are describing a window that does, such as 11 PM to 2 AM, split it into two ranges: 11 PM to 12 AM, and 12 AM to 2 AM.

You can also set different schedules for different days, so weekends can be looser than school or work days.

03

Limit Instagram time per day

  • App Pause
  • Usage Based
  • 2 minutes

Usage Based mode counts how long you spend in Instagram each day and blocks it when you reach your limit. The counter resets at midnight. This is the closest thing to "I just want to use it less" and the easiest place to start.

  1. Go to Reducers → Apps and tap +.
  2. Name the group and select Instagram.
  3. Choose Usage Based and set your daily minutes.
  4. Optional: turn off the uniform setting and give each weekday its own limit, such as 15 minutes on weekdays and 60 on weekends. A day set to 0 is fully blocked.
  5. Tap Done.

Going further: only 6 to 9 PM, and only for one hour

Curbox lets you put the same app in two groups if they use different rule types, and it checks every rule each time you open the app. Stacking a Time Based group with a Usage Based group gives you a window and a cap.

What does not stack
Two Usage groups on the same app, or two Time Based groups, do not combine; only the last one you configured counts. Mix one of each type instead.

04

Block only Reels

  • Short-Form Video
  • Time or count
  • 2 minutes

For a lot of people the problem is not Instagram, it is the Reels tab. Curbox can stop Reels after a daily time limit or after a number of videos while messages, Stories and your profile keep working.

  1. Go to Reducers → Short-Form Video Content.
  2. Turn on the main switch at the top right.
  3. Choose Time Based or Reel Count Based and set the limit. Set it low to make Reels effectively off.
  4. Optional: allow Reels opened from your DM inbox, so a video a friend sends you still plays.

The limit is shared across Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels, so you cannot dodge it by switching apps. There is a full walkthrough on the block Instagram Reels guide.

05

Hide the feed and Explore, keep DMs

  • UI Hider
  • F-Droid / GitHub build
  • 1 minute

If you use Instagram to talk to friends, blocking the whole app is overkill. Curbox's UI Hider covers parts of the screen inside other apps. It ships with ready-made Instagram rules you can switch on separately:

  • Hide the home feed except the Following tab. You reach Following by tapping the cursive "Instagram" text at the top.
  • Hide the Explore tab.
  1. Go to Reducers → UI Hider and turn on Enable UI Hider.
  2. Switch on the Instagram rules you want.

Availability
UI Hider is included in the F-Droid and GitHub builds of Curbox, not the Playstore build. It is still marked experimental, and an Instagram update can occasionally break a rule until it is fixed.

06

Close the browser loophole

  • Keywords and Websites
  • 1 minute

Once the app is blocked, the next move your brain will try is instagram.com in Chrome. Curbox watches the address bar of supported browsers and shows the same pause screen.

  1. Go to Reducers → Keywords And Websites and tap +.
  2. Add instagram.com to block the whole site, or instagram.com/reels to block only Reels pages.
  3. Tap Done.

This works in Chrome, Brave, Firefox, Opera and several privacy browsers. For anything not on that list, add the browser itself to an App Pause group. The website blocking guide has the full keyword syntax and a pattern tester.

Make the block hard enough to keep

A block you can dismiss in one tap mostly teaches you to tap. Each App Pause and Short-Form Video group has its own warning screen, so you can make Instagram strict while leaving other apps gentle.

  • Never Unlock: no button at all. Good for recipe 01.
  • Wait to unlock: a fixed or unpredictable wait before you can continue. Even a short delay breaks autopilot.
  • Require effort: scan a QR code or barcode you keep in another room, tap an NFC tag, solve math, type a sentence, or write down why you are opening Instagram.
  • Proceed limits: cap how many times you can get through in a time window.
  • Link to Focus Mode: for example, no unlocks until you have focused for an hour that day.

The F-Droid and GitHub builds also have anti-uninstall protection, so removing Curbox in a weak moment takes more than a long-press. Details are in the unlock challenges guide.

What about Android's built-in Digital Wellbeing?

Most Android phones already include an app timer under Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls. You can set a daily limit for Instagram, and when it runs out the icon is greyed out until midnight. If all you need is a soft reminder, it is worth trying first, and it costs nothing to set up.

Digital Wellbeing app timer

  • One daily timer per app
  • Removed from the same screen in a few taps
  • No way to block only Reels or the feed
  • No choice of what happens at the limit

Curbox

  • Time windows, daily caps, or every open, and combinations
  • You choose the friction: none, a wait, a task, or no way through
  • Reels limits shared across Instagram, YouTube and Facebook
  • Browser blocking for instagram.com

The honest difference is friction. Digital Wellbeing is designed to be easy to change, which is the right call for a system setting but means the timer is easy to switch off the moment it bothers you. If you have already turned it off a few times, that is the sign you need something you set up in a calmer moment and cannot undo in a second. Questions

Can I block Instagram on Android without deleting it?

Yes. An app blocker like Curbox leaves Instagram installed and shows a pause screen when you open it outside the rules you set, so your account, messages and saved posts stay untouched.

Does Android have a built-in way to limit Instagram?

Yes. Digital Wellbeing lets you set a daily app timer for Instagram. When the timer runs out the icon is paused until midnight. The timer can be removed from the same settings screen in a few taps, so it works best as a reminder rather than a hard block.

Can I block only Instagram Reels and keep messages?

Yes. Curbox's Short-Form Video reducer blocks Reels after a daily time or video-count limit while the rest of Instagram, including DMs, keeps working. You can also allow Reels that friends send you in DMs.

Will Instagram know I blocked it?

No. Curbox works on your phone through Android's Accessibility Service and has no internet permission. Instagram only sees that you opened the app less.

Can I make the block harder to get around?

Yes. Set the warning screen to Never Unlock, or require an effort such as scanning a QR code you keep in another room. The F-Droid and GitHub builds of Curbox also include anti-uninstall protection.

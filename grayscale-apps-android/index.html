Grayscale only the apps that hook you.

Android can turn your whole screen black and white. That is fine for bedtime, less fine when you need your camera or maps. Here is how to gray out just Instagram, YouTube or TikTok and leave everything else in color.

Why color matters

Social and video apps are designed to be bright. Red notification badges, saturated thumbnails, colorful reels: all of it competes for your eye. Taking the color away does not block anything, but it makes the feed look flatter and a little boring, and for many people that is enough to close the app sooner. It is one of the gentlest habit tools there is, because nothing stops you. The app just stops being as pretty.

The problem is that whole-screen grayscale punishes the useful parts of your phone too. Photos look wrong, maps are harder to read, and you end up switching it off within a day. Per-app grayscale fixes that. Try it below.

The built-in options (whole screen)

Stock Android has three ways to go grayscale. None of them can target a single app, but they cost nothing and need no install, so they are worth knowing. Menu names vary by manufacturer; these paths are for Pixel and close-to-stock phones, and Samsung and others have equivalents in similar places.

Bedtime modeScheduled

Grayscale at night

Digital Wellbeing can turn the screen gray on a schedule as part of Bedtime mode.

Settings › Digital Wellbeing & parental controls › Bedtime mode › Screen options › Grayscale

Good for winding down. It applies to the whole phone, only during the hours you set.

AccessibilityAny time

Color correction set to grayscale

Settings › Accessibility › Color and motion › Color correction › Grayscale

Meant for people who see color differently, but it works as an always-on gray mode. You can add a shortcut to toggle it quickly. On Samsung, look under Accessibility for color adjustment or color filters.

Developer optionsHidden

Simulate color space: Monochromacy

First unlock Developer options by tapping the build number seven times (Settings › About phone).

Settings › System › Developer options › Simulate color space › Monochromacy

A testing tool, not a feature, so treat it as a quick experiment rather than a long-term setup.

If any of these is enough for you, great. If you found yourself turning it off to take a photo, per-app grayscale is the fix.

Per-app grayscale with Curbox

Curbox's Grayscale Filter switches the screen to black and white the moment you open a chosen app, and back to color when you leave. Changing the display's color mode is a protected system setting, so Curbox uses Shizuku, a free helper app, to get that one permission without root. Shizuku is the only extra step, and on Android 11 or later you do not need a computer.

Part 1 · Install

  1. Install Curbox and finish setup

    Get it from the downloads page (Google Play, F-Droid or GitHub). Grant the three core permissions during onboarding: display over other apps, notifications, and the App Blocker accessibility service. Setup takes a few minutes.

  2. Install Shizuku

    Shizuku is on the Play Store and on its GitHub releases page. It is free and open source.

Part 2 · Start Shizuku with Wireless Debugging

  1. Turn on Developer options

    Settings › About phone, then tap Build number seven times until you see a confirmation.

  2. Connect to Wi-Fi and enable Wireless debugging

    Settings › System › Developer options › Wireless debugging, and switch it on. Wireless debugging needs a Wi-Fi connection, though it does not need the internet beyond that.

  3. Pair Shizuku

    Open Shizuku and choose to start via Wireless debugging, then tap Pairing. Shizuku asks you to open Wireless debugging and tap "Pair device with pairing code". A code appears; enter it in Shizuku's notification. Split-screen or the notification shade makes this easier.

  4. Tap Start

    Back in Shizuku, tap Start. The top of the app should now say Shizuku is running. You can switch Wireless debugging off again afterwards if you like.

    On Android 10 or earlier, Shizuku needs to be started once from a computer with adb, or with root. Shizuku's own instructions show the command.

Part 3 · Choose your gray apps

  1. Grant Curbox the Shizuku permission

    In Curbox, go to Reducers › Grayscale Filter. You will see a "Shizuku Permission Required" card. Tap Grant Permission and allow it in the Shizuku prompt.

  2. Create a group

    Tap the + button, give the group a name such as "Scroll apps", select the apps that should turn gray, and tap Done.

  3. Open one of them

    The filter applies immediately. Open Instagram or YouTube and the screen goes black and white; switch to your camera and the color comes back.

Full reference: the Grayscale Filter docs.

Questions

Can Android make only one app grayscale?

Not with built-in settings. Android's grayscale options (Bedtime mode, Developer options, Accessibility color correction) change the whole screen. To gray out only chosen apps you need an app such as Curbox, which switches grayscale on when those apps open and off when you leave.

Why does Curbox need Shizuku for grayscale?

Changing the screen's color mode is a protected system setting. Shizuku is a free helper that lets an app use that kind of permission without root. Curbox only needs it for the Grayscale Filter; every other feature works without it.

Do I need a computer to set up Shizuku?

Not on Android 11 or later. Shizuku can start itself using Wireless Debugging, which is built into the phone. On older versions you need a one-time adb command from a computer.

Does grayscale actually reduce phone use?

For many people it makes colorful, attention-grabbing apps feel flatter and easier to put down. It is a gentle nudge rather than a block, so it works best paired with a limit if you need one.

Will I need to set up Shizuku again after a restart?

Often, yes. With Wireless Debugging, Shizuku usually has to be started again after the phone reboots. Open Shizuku and tap start; the Curbox permission stays granted.

What is urge surfing?

Urge surfing is a way of dealing with a craving by riding it out. You do not give in, and you do not grit your teeth and fight it. You notice it, name it, and let it pass. The idea is often described with a wave: an urge rises, peaks and then fades on its own, usually within a few minutes, as long as you do not feed it.

Phone cravings fit this pattern well. The pull to open Instagram after you hit your limit feels strong in the moment, but it is rarely about anything specific. It is boredom, or a pause in a task, or a small itch of anxiety looking for somewhere to go. Give it three minutes and something else to do, and it often loses its grip.

The goal is not to never feel the urge. It is to notice that you can feel it and still choose.

How urge surfing works in Curbox, step by step

The whole routine takes about four minutes and happens right on the block screen.

  1. 01Ask for helpTap "I'm struggling" on any block screen. After 3 unlocks in a row, Curbox offers it on its own.
  2. 02Rate itHow strong is the craving right now, from 0 to 10? Be honest; there is no wrong number.
  3. 03ReflectTake a moment with the feeling before doing anything about it.
  4. 04Three minutesPlay a calm block puzzle with no score, or move your body: squats, stairs, a fast walk.
  5. 05Rate againCheck in with the craving now. Often it has dropped, and seeing that is the lesson.

Why rate the craving twice?

Rating a craving before and after is the part that teaches you something. In the moment, an urge feels like it will last forever. Seeing your own numbers, say a 7 that became a 3 after three minutes of squats, is better evidence than any article that waiting works.

Curbox keeps these ratings in a "Your cravings" history, which shows how much your cravings dropped on average. It stays on your phone, because the Curbox app has no internet permission, and you can clear it whenever you like.

Puzzle or movement: which should you pick?

The calm block puzzle

Something for your hands and eyes that asks nothing of you. There is no score, no streak and no feed, so there is nothing to get hooked on. Good on a bus, in bed, or anywhere you cannot get up.

or

Moving your body

Squats, a flight of stairs, a fast walk around the block. Getting up changes your surroundings and your state at the same time. Good when you are at a desk and the craving is really restlessness.

Neither is better. Try both over a week and notice which one leaves your second rating lower.

Urge surfing works best with a little friction

Urge surfing is about the moment of choice, so it pairs well with anything that creates one. A short delay timer on the block screen gives the urge a few seconds to start fading before you even see the options. A mindful reminder with your own words can make you pause before the block. And if you keep losing the battle on the same app, a key in another room, like a QR code or an NFC tag, turns the next craving into a walk.

Urge surfing questions

What is urge surfing?

Urge surfing is a way of handling a craving by noticing it and waiting it out instead of acting on it or fighting it. The idea is that a craving behaves like a wave: it builds, peaks and fades, often within a few minutes, if you give it time.

Is there an app for urge surfing on phone cravings?

Yes. Curbox for Android has Urge Surfing built into its block screen. Tap "I'm struggling" to rate the craving, take a moment to reflect, spend 3 minutes on a calm block puzzle or some movement, then rate it again.

How do I ride out the urge to check my phone?

Give it a few minutes and something else to do with your hands or body. Name how strong the urge is, do a short calm activity, then check in again. Most urges are weaker on the second rating than the first. Curbox walks you through exactly this loop.

When does Curbox offer urge surfing?

When the option is on, block screens show an "I'm struggling" button. Curbox can also offer help automatically after you unlock 3 times in a row, even if the button is turned off.

Does Curbox keep a record of my cravings?

Yes, on your phone. A "Your cravings" history shows how much your cravings dropped on average between the first and second rating. You can clear it at any time. The Curbox app has no internet permission, so this history never leaves your device.

Is urge surfing a treatment for addiction?

Curbox is not a medical tool and does not treat anything. Urge surfing in Curbox is a simple self-help routine for everyday phone habits. If you are struggling with compulsive behaviour, talk to a professional.

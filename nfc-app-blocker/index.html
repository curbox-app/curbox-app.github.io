Feature · NFC tags

An NFC app blocker that lives on a sticker.

Curbox turns a cheap NFC tag into a physical switch for your attention. Tap a tag on your desk to start Focus Mode, or make a tag in another room the only key to Instagram. It is a free, open source NFC app blocker for Android, and it works with tags you can buy by the dozen.

One NFC tag, two different jobs

People search for "NFC app blocker" meaning one of two things, and Curbox does both. They are set up in different places and you can use both at once.

Job 1 · the key

Tap to unlock a blocked app

NFC is one of the unlock challenges on the Curbox pause screen. When a blocked app is opened, the proceed button only appears after you tap the tag you registered.

  1. Reducers → Apps, open or create a group.
  2. Configure Warning Screen.
  3. Unlock Challenge: Require effort to unlock, then NFC Tag.
  4. Register your tag by tapping it, then save.
Job 2 · the switch

Tap to start or stop Focus Mode

Curbox can write a Focus Mode action onto a writable tag. Tapping the tag against your phone then runs that action, no app opening required.

  1. Pick a Focus Group: the apps and sites to block.
  2. Pick a duration for the session.
  3. Pick the action: Start, Stop or Toggle.
  4. Hold a blank tag to the phone to write it.

What do Start, Stop and Toggle do on an NFC focus tag?

Each focus tag holds one action. Choosing the right one depends on how you want the tag to fit into your day.

ActionWhat a tap doesGood for
Start focusStarts a session with the group and duration on the tag.A tag on your desk or library card holder. Sit down, tap, work.
Stop focusEnds the current session, if the session allows quitting.A tag by the front door, so leaving work mode is a deliberate act.
Toggle focusStarts a session if none is running, stops it if one is.One tag that does both, when you trust yourself with it.

One rule matters here: if a session is set so it cannot be quit early, a tag cannot end it either. Tapping Stop or Toggle during a locked session does nothing. That keeps the tag from becoming a cheat code on a hard afternoon. Read more about session options in the Focus Mode guide.

Which NFC tags should you buy?

You do not need anything special. Here is what to look for, based on what is commonly sold and widely used with Android phones.

Shopping label · NFC tags for Curbox
FormStickers or small round discs. Keyring fobs and cards also work.
ChipNTAG213, NTAG215 or NTAG216 are common, inexpensive and readable by most Android phones. Any of them holds far more than a focus action needs.
WritableRequired for focus tags, because Curbox writes the action to the tag. Do not lock the tag with another app, or it cannot be rewritten later.
SurfaceTags on metal often read poorly. Buy "on-metal" tags if you plan to stick one on a fridge or laptop.
QuantityBuy a pack. You will want one for the desk, one for the door, and a spare for when you change your mind.
PhoneYour phone needs NFC. Look for an NFC toggle in connection settings before ordering.

Already have a transit card, a work badge or a payment card with NFC? Those can be fine as an unlock key, but they are not writable, so they cannot be focus tags. And a key you carry everywhere adds no distance, which is the whole point.

Where to stick your tags

The tag is only as useful as its location. A few setups that fit real routines:

Under the deskA Start tag where your hand rests. Sitting down to work becomes one tap.
On the front doorA Stop tag, so ending focus means standing up and leaving the desk.
In the kitchenAn unlock tag for social apps. Every scroll session starts with a short walk.
On your study bagA Start tag for library days. Arrive, tap, and the phone goes quiet.
By the bed, for morningsA Start tag with a short session, so the first 30 minutes of the day stay off the feed.
At your partner's deskAn unlock tag someone else holds. Asking out loud is a strong pause.

Why a physical tag helps you focus

Starting focus in an app takes several taps, and every tap is a chance to notice a notification and drift off. A tag compresses the decision into one physical gesture you can attach to a place. Over a few weeks, sitting at the desk and tapping the tag tends to feel like one action, the same way putting your keys in a bowl does.

Unlocking works the other way round. A button on the pause screen is right under your thumb. A tag in the kitchen is not. The extra walk gives a craving time to pass, and when it does not, you have still made a real choice.

NFC app blocker questions

Can I use an NFC tag to block apps on Android?

Yes. Curbox supports NFC in two ways. As an unlock challenge, a blocked app only opens after you tap a tag you registered. As a focus tag, tapping a tag starts, stops or toggles a Focus Mode session with the group and duration you chose.

What NFC tags work with Curbox?

Focus tags need a writable tag, because Curbox writes the focus action onto it. Common, inexpensive NFC sticker tags such as the NTAG213, NTAG215 and NTAG216 types are widely used with Android phones. For unlocking, you register a tag you already have.

Can I stop a focus session early by tapping the tag?

Only if the session allows it. If you set a session so it cannot be quit early, the tag cannot end it either. That keeps the tag from becoming an easy way out.

Does my phone need NFC?

Yes. Most mid-range and flagship Android phones include NFC, but some budget models do not. Check for an NFC toggle in your phone's connection settings before you buy tags.

Is an NFC tag better than a QR code for unlocking?

They do the same job. NFC is faster and works in the dark; a QR code or barcode costs nothing because you can use one you already own. Both work best when the key is in another room.

Phone addiction test: a 15-question self-check

This phone addiction test is a short self-check with 15 statements. You score each one from 0 to 2, add up the points, and read what your total means. Tap an answer for each statement. You get your score and a plan at the end. It takes about three minutes.

Before you take the phone addiction test

This test shows how much control you have over your phone use. It is not a medical test and it cannot give a diagnosis. "Phone addiction" and "smartphone addiction" are not formal diagnoses in the DSM-5. Use your score as a starting point for change, not as a label.

Think about the last two weeks, not your best or worst day. Answer honestly. Nobody else sees your answers.

How to score each statement

  • 0 points: Never or almost never.
  • 1 point: Sometimes (a few times a week).
  • 2 points: Often (most days).

Take the phone addiction test

Read each statement. Write down 0, 1 or 2. Then add up your points. The lowest possible total is 0 and the highest is 30.

    What your score means

    0 to 8: Mostly in control

    Your phone use seems to fit your life. You may still have one or two habits that you want to change, such as phone use in bed. Pick one and work on it.

    9 to 17: Some loss of control

    The phone takes more of your time and attention than you want. This is the most common result for people who look for a test like this. Small changes in your setup can make a large difference. Start with notifications and one app limit.

    18 to 30: Strong loss of control

    Your phone use probably affects your sleep, mood, work or relationships. This does not mean something is wrong with you. Apps are designed to hold attention. It does mean that it is time for a clear plan, and maybe for support from other people.

    The bands are a guide, not hard lines. A score of 16 and a score of 19 are close. Look at which statements scored 2. Those show where to start.

    Read your answers, not only your score

    The statements fall into groups. Your high-scoring group tells you what kind of change will help most.

    • Statements 1, 6 and 12 (control): Hard limits and timers help most. See how to break phone addiction.
    • Statements 2, 3 and 14 (checking): Fewer notifications and a pause before apps open help most. See compulsive phone checking.
    • Statements 5, 7 and 11 (mood): Learn what feelings send you to the phone. See dopamine and phone addiction and phone addiction and anxiety.
    • Statements 4 and 13 (safety and sleep): Set fixed phone-free times, such as at night and when you drive.
    • Statements 8, 9, 10 and 15 (life impact): Talk with the people around you and agree on phone-free times together.

    Safety first. If you scored 1 or 2 on statement 13, change that habit before anything else. Put the phone out of reach or turn on a driving mode before you drive.

    Take the test again later

    A single score tells you where you are today. Two scores tell you if your changes work. This page remembers your last score in your browser, so you can compare the next time.

    1. Choose one or two changes based on your high-scoring statements.
    2. Keep the changes for two weeks.
    3. Take the test again.
    4. Compare the two totals and the statements that changed.

    If your score does not go down, the changes may be too weak or too many at once. Try one stronger change instead. You can also compare your result with the signs of phone addiction list.

    Questions

    Is this phone addiction test a diagnosis?

    No. It is a self-check to help you think about your phone use. Phone addiction is not a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5. If phone use causes serious problems, talk to a doctor or mental health professional.

    What is a good score on the phone addiction test?

    A score of 0 to 8 suggests that your phone use is mostly in control. A score of 9 to 17 suggests some loss of control, and 18 to 30 suggests a strong loss of control.

    How often should I take the test?

    Take it once now, then again after two weeks of changes. Compare the totals and the statements that changed to see if your plan works.

    Can I use this test for my teenager?

    A teenager can take it for themselves, and it can start a useful talk. Do not use it as proof against them. Let them read their own result and choose one change.

    My score is high. What should I do first?

    Look at the statements where you scored 2 and pick one area, such as notifications or phone use in bed. Make one clear change there, keep it for two weeks, and take the test again.

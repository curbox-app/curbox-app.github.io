Screen addiction · Screen time limits

How much screen time is too much?

Screen time is too much when it pushes out sleep, movement, work, school or people, or when you cannot cut back when you want to. There is no single number of hours that fits every person. This page shows you how to judge your own screen time and set a limit that makes sense.

Is there a safe number of hours?

For adults, there is no official number of hours that counts as too much screen time. The effect of screen time depends on what you do, when you do it, and what it replaces. Two hours of video calls with family are not the same as two hours of late-night short videos.

For young children, many health organizations give age-based advice. In general, this advice suggests very little screen time for babies and toddlers and short limits for young children. For older children and teens, it suggests consistent family rules. Check the current advice from your local health service or your child's doctor, because guidance changes over time and differs by country.

So instead of one number, use three questions:

  1. What does the screen time replace? If it takes the place of sleep, exercise, meals with others or work, it is a problem.
  2. How does it feel after? If you often feel tired, empty or annoyed after a session, the content does not serve you.
  3. Can you stop when you want to? If you often use your phone longer than you planned, the habit is in control.

Signs that your screen time is too much

Look at your life, not only at the screen time number. These signs suggest that screen use is too much for you right now:

  • You go to bed later than you planned because of your phone.
  • You feel tired eyes, a stiff neck or headaches after long sessions.
  • You often put off work or study to scroll.
  • People close to you complain about your phone use.
  • You move much less than you want to.
  • You feel anxious or restless when you cannot check your phone.
  • You set limits and break them again and again.

If many of these are true, read about the signs of phone addiction or take the phone addiction self-check.

Not all screen time is equal

A total screen time number mixes very different things. Split your time into groups before you decide what to cut:

  • Necessary: work, school, banking, maps, calls.
  • Social: messages and calls with people you know.
  • Active: learning, creating, reading long articles, planning.
  • Passive: endless feeds, autoplay video, short-form video.

Passive screen time is usually the best group to reduce first. It is easy to lose track of, and it often leaves you feeling worse. Feeds and autoplay are designed with no natural end. See why apps are addictive.

Time of day matters

Screen time late at night can delay sleep, because light and stimulating content keep your brain alert. Screen time first thing in the morning can set a distracted tone for the day. The same hour of use can have a different effect at different times. For more, see phone use and sleep.

How to find your own screen time limit

  1. Track your screen time for one normal week. Do not change anything yet.
  2. Write down your daily average and your top three apps.
  3. Mark each top app as necessary, social, active or passive.
  4. Pick one passive app to reduce first.
  5. Set a daily limit for that app that is lower than your average, but not extreme.
  6. Keep the limit for two weeks.
  7. Review how you feel and lower the limit again if it helps.

Small, steady cuts are easier to keep than a sudden drop. Our guide to screen time goals explains how to set targets that you can actually reach.

If phone use causes serious problems in your life, talk to a doctor or a mental health professional. If you are worried about a child's screen time, talk to their doctor about the right limits for their age.

Questions

How many hours of screen time is too much for an adult?

There is no official number for adults. Screen time is too much when it pushes out sleep, movement, work or people, or when you cannot cut back when you want to. What you do on the screen and when you do it matter more than the total hours.

How much screen time is too much for a child?

Many health organizations give age-based advice for children, with very little screen time for the youngest children and clear family rules for older ones. Guidance differs by country and changes over time, so check with your local health service or your child's doctor.

Does work screen time count?

It counts toward eye strain and time spent sitting, so take regular breaks. But it is different from passive scrolling. When you decide what to cut, start with passive screen time such as feeds and autoplay video.

Is screen time before bed bad?

Late screen use can delay sleep, because the light and the content keep your brain alert. Many people sleep better when they stop screens some time before bed and charge the phone outside the bedroom.

How do I cut down my screen time?

Track one normal week first. Then pick one passive app, set a daily limit lower than your average, and keep it for two weeks. Lower the limit again if it helps. Small, steady cuts are easier to keep than a sudden drop.

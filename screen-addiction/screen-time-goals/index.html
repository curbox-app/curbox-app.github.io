Screen time goals: how to set goals you can keep

Good screen time goals start from your real usage, focus on the apps and hours that matter, and change in small steps. A goal like "no phone" is easy to write and hard to keep. This page shows how to set screen time goals that still work after the first week.

What makes a good screen time goal?

A good screen time goal is specific, measurable and a little below your current use. It names an app or a time of day, it has a number, and you can check it at the end of the day. "Use my phone less" is a wish. "Thirty minutes of short videos a day, and none after 10 PM" is a goal.

A good goal also fits your life. If your work needs the phone, a low total screen time goal will fail on the first busy day. In that case, a goal for one app or one hour of the day is more useful.

The aim is control, not zero. Screen time for calls, maps, learning or a film with friends is not a problem. Your goals should target the use that you regret, not all use.

How to set screen time goals in five steps

  1. Measure first. Use your phone normally for one week and look at the data. Note your daily total, your top three apps, and your busiest hours.
  2. Find the regret. Ask which use you regret at the end of the day. That is usually a feed, a game or late-night use.
  3. Choose one goal. Pick one app or one time window. One goal is easier to keep than five.
  4. Set the first number. Choose a number a little below your current use. For example, if you use an app for 90 minutes a day, start with 75.
  5. Review each week. If you kept the goal on most days, make it a little stricter. If you did not, find out why before you change it.
Why small steps work. A small cut feels possible, so you keep it. Each success makes the next step easier. A big cut often breaks in the first week, and many people then drop the goal completely.

Types of screen time goals

There are several kinds of goal. You can mix them, but start with one.

Time goals

A daily limit for one app or a group of apps. For example, "one hour of social media a day". Time goals are easy to measure and easy to enforce with a limit.

Time window goals

Rules about when, not how much. For example, "no social media before 9 AM" or "no phone in bed". These goals often help sleep and mornings more than a time limit does. See how to stop using your phone in bed.

Count goals

A limit on the number of things. For example, "twenty short videos a day" or "check email three times a day". Counts work well for short-form video, where time is hard to feel. Read about Reels and Shorts addiction.

Replacement goals

A goal for what you do instead. For example, "read ten pages before bed" or "walk after lunch". These goals fill the gap that a limit leaves. See things to do instead of scrolling.

Focus goals

A goal for time without distraction. For example, "two focus sessions of 25 minutes each workday". These goals are useful for study and work. See phone distraction at work.

What is a reasonable screen time goal?

There is no single correct number for adults. Your work, your health and your reasons for phone use all matter. A goal that is right for a student before exams is wrong for a nurse on night shifts.

Instead of a fixed number, ask two questions about your screen time. Does it stop you from doing things you care about, such as sleep, work, exercise or time with people? Do you often use the phone longer than you planned? If both answers are no, your screen time is probably fine. If either answer is yes, start with a goal there. How to keep your screen time goals

  • Make the goal automatic. Use a limit or a schedule so that the phone holds the rule for you.
  • Keep the number visible. A widget with today's total reminds you of the goal each time you unlock the phone.
  • Plan for bad days. Decide on a softer rule for sick days or stressful weeks, instead of no rule.
  • Count wins, not perfect days. Five good days out of seven is a success.
  • Change one thing at a time. After a missed week, fix the one trigger that caused it. Read what to do after a relapse.

Questions

What is a good screen time goal?

A good screen time goal is specific, measurable and a little below your current use. It names an app or a time of day and has a number. For example: thirty minutes of short videos a day, and none after 10 PM.

How much screen time should an adult aim for?

There is no single correct number for adults. Ask if your screen time stops you from doing things you care about, and if you often use the phone longer than you planned. If so, set a goal for that use.

Should I set a goal for total screen time or for single apps?

For most people, a goal for one app or one time of day works better. Total screen time includes useful things like maps and calls. A goal on the use you regret is easier to keep and more helpful.

How fast should I lower my screen time goal?

Start a little below your current use and review once a week. If you kept the goal on most days, make it a little stricter. If you did not, find the reason before you change the number.

What should I do if I miss my screen time goal?

Look at the day and the hour when it happened and ask what triggered it. Then change one rule for that trigger. One missed day is normal and does not reset your progress.

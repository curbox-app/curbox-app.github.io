Healthy screen time for adults: habits that actually work

Healthy screen time for adults is screen use that you choose and that fits around sleep, movement and people. You can also stop it when you want to. Most adults need screens for work and daily life, so the goal is balance, not zero. This page gives you clear daily habits and a simple weekly plan.

What does healthy screen time look like?

Healthy screen time is not a fixed number of hours. It is a pattern. You use screens for a purpose, you take breaks, and screens do not take the place of sleep, exercise or time with other people. When you put the phone down, you do not feel a strong pull to pick it up again.

Adults have a special problem. Work often needs many hours on a computer, and then the phone fills the time after work too. The total can get very high without any single choice that feels wrong. A healthy pattern separates the screen time you need from the screen time you drift into.

Use these four areas to check your balance: sleep, body, attention and people. The sections below give habits for each area.

Screen time habits for sleep

  • Stop screens some time before bed. Many people find that 30 to 60 minutes helps.
  • Charge your phone outside the bedroom.
  • Use a normal alarm clock, so you do not need the phone next to your bed.
  • Turn on Do Not Disturb at night on a schedule.
  • Do not start a new episode or a new feed late at night.

Late screen use can delay sleep, and less sleep makes it harder to control phone habits the next day. For more, read phone use and sleep.

Screen time habits for your eyes and body

  • Follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 meters) away for 20 seconds.
  • Hold the phone at eye level when you can, instead of bending your neck.
  • Stand up and move at least once an hour during desk work.
  • Keep the screen at a comfortable distance and brightness.
  • Blink on purpose during long sessions. People tend to blink less when they look at screens.

If your eyes often feel dry or tired, see our guide to digital eye strain.

Screen time habits for focus and people

Focus

  • Turn off notifications from apps that are not from people.
  • Check email and messages at set times, not all day.
  • Do your most important work in a block with the phone out of reach.
  • Use one screen at a time. Do not scroll while you watch something else.

Read more about focus at work in phone distraction at work.

People

  • Keep meals free of phones.
  • Put the phone away when someone talks to you.
  • Use screens to connect with people, such as calls, more than to watch strangers.

If phone use already causes tension at home, see phubbing.

A simple weekly plan for healthy screen time

Do not change everything at once. Add one habit each week:

  1. Week 1: Track your screen time. Do not change anything. Note your top three apps.
  2. Week 2: Turn off notifications that are not from people.
  3. Week 3: Move your phone charger out of the bedroom.
  4. Week 4: Set a daily limit on the one app that takes the most passive time.
  5. Week 5: Add one phone-free block each day for work, a hobby or exercise.

If a week feels hard, stay on it longer. A habit that you keep is worth more than five habits that you drop. To decide on your own numbers, see how much screen time is too much.

Questions

What is a healthy amount of screen time for adults?

There is no official number for adults. Healthy screen time is screen use that you choose, that fits around sleep, movement and people, and that you can stop when you want to. Many adults need many hours for work, so focus on cutting passive screen time first.

Does screen time for work count as unhealthy?

Work screen time can still cause eye strain and long periods of sitting, so take regular breaks and follow the 20-20-20 rule. But it is different from passive scrolling. Most adults get more benefit from cutting feeds and autoplay video than from cutting work time.

What is the 20-20-20 rule?

Every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 meters) away for 20 seconds. It gives your eyes a short rest from close focus on a screen.

How can I balance screen time and real life?

Add one habit at a time. Track your use and turn off notifications that are not from people. Charge the phone outside the bedroom. Then limit the app that takes most of your passive time. Keep each habit for a week before you add the next one.

Is it bad to use my phone right before bed?

Late screen use can delay sleep, because the light and the content keep your brain alert. Many people sleep better if they stop screens some time before bed and keep the phone outside the bedroom.

Digital detox guide: how to plan a break from screens that lasts

A digital detox is a planned break from some or all screens, for a set time. It can be one evening or a full week. This guide helps you pick the right type of detox, plan it, and keep the good parts after it ends.

What is a digital detox?

A digital detox is a set period when you stop or reduce the use of digital devices, such as your phone, social media or games. The goal is to reset your habits and to notice how you feel without constant screens. A detox is a tool, not a cure.

Many people start a detox because they feel tired, distracted or anxious after long hours on their phone. Others want to test if they can go without a specific app. Both are good reasons. A detox shows you which habits you miss and which ones you do not miss at all.

A detox does not need to be extreme. A short, realistic break that you finish is more useful than a big plan that you stop on day two.

Types of digital detox

Choose the type that fits your life and your problem:

Full detox

No phone, computer or TV for a short time, such as a weekend. This works best on a trip or a holiday, when you do not need your phone for work. Tell people how to reach you in an emergency.

App detox

You keep your phone, but you stop one or two apps, such as Instagram or TikTok. This is the most practical type for most people, because calls, maps and messages still work.

Time-based detox

You stop screens at set times every day, for example the first hour after you wake up and the last hour before bed. See stop checking your phone in the morning and stop using your phone in bed.

Content detox

You keep an app but remove its most addictive part, such as the short video feed. You can still message friends and search, but the endless scroll is gone.

How to plan a digital detox step by step

  1. Write down why you want a detox. Use one clear sentence.
  2. Check your current screen time for a few days, so you know your start point.
  3. Pick the type of detox and the length. A first detox of one to seven days is realistic.
  4. Choose the apps or devices that the detox covers. Write them down.
  5. Decide what is allowed, such as calls, maps or banking.
  6. Tell friends, family or your team, so they know why you reply slowly.
  7. Plan what you will do with the free time. Make a list of offline activities.
  8. Set up blocks or limits on your phone before the detox starts.
  9. Pick a day to review how it went.

Step 7 is the one that people skip most often. If you remove the phone and leave an empty gap, the gap pulls you back. Our list of things to do instead of scrolling can help you fill it.

What to expect in the first days

The first one or two days are often the hardest. You may reach for your phone many times without a reason. You may feel bored, restless or a little anxious. Some people call these feelings phone addiction withdrawal. For most people they are mild and fade as new routines form.

Use these tips when a craving comes:

  • Name the feeling: "I am bored" or "I want to know if someone replied."
  • Wait a few minutes. Most cravings get weaker if you do not act on them.
  • Move your body. A short walk or a few stretches change your state quickly.
  • Go back to your list of offline activities.
Not medical advice. This page is general information. It is not medical advice and it does not replace a diagnosis. If phone use causes serious problems in your life, talk to a doctor or a mental health professional. If you feel very anxious or low when you are away from your phone, or the feelings do not fade, get support from a professional. If you are in crisis, contact your local emergency or crisis support service.

How to keep the benefits after a detox

Many people go back to old habits a few days after a detox. This is normal, and it does not mean the detox failed. To keep the benefits, turn the detox into smaller daily rules:

  • Keep one rule from the detox, such as no phone in the bedroom.
  • Do not reinstall an app that you did not miss.
  • Keep daily limits on the apps that you brought back.
  • Repeat a short detox when you notice old habits return.

If old habits come back fast, read our guide on relapse after a phone detox. A detox is a reset. Long-term change comes from the setup around you.

Questions

What is a digital detox?

A digital detox is a set period when you stop or reduce the use of digital devices, such as your phone, social media or games. It helps you reset habits and notice how you feel with fewer screens. It can last one evening or several days.

How long should a digital detox last?

There is no correct length. For a first detox, one to seven days is realistic. A short detox that you finish teaches you more than a long one that you stop early. You can repeat it later or make it longer.

Do I need to give up my phone for a digital detox?

No. Most people do an app detox or a time-based detox. You keep calls, messages and maps, and you stop the apps or the times that cause problems.

Why do I feel restless during a digital detox?

Your brain is used to frequent new input from the phone. When that input stops, you can feel bored, restless or a little anxious. For most people these feelings are mild and fade after a few days. Plan offline activities to fill the gap.

How do I stop old habits after a digital detox?

Keep one or two rules from the detox as daily habits, such as no phone in bed. Keep limits on apps that you brought back, and do not reinstall apps you did not miss. Repeat a short detox when old habits return.

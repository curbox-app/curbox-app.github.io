Digital eye strain: symptoms, causes and relief

Digital eye strain is the tired, dry or sore feeling in your eyes after long periods of screen use. It is common, and it usually goes away when you rest your eyes. The best fixes are regular breaks, the 20-20-20 rule and a better screen setup.

What is digital eye strain?

Digital eye strain is a group of eye and vision problems that come from long use of screens such as phones, tablets and computers. It is also called computer vision syndrome. The symptoms are usually temporary. They often get better when you take a break from the screen.

Eye strain is not the same as screen addiction. You can get eye strain from a long day of necessary work on a laptop. But heavy phone use, especially long scroll sessions, is a common cause. So less unplanned screen time often helps your eyes too.

Common symptoms of digital eye strain

  • Tired, sore or burning eyes.
  • Dry eyes, or eyes that water.
  • Blurred vision, or trouble when you change focus from near to far.
  • Headaches, often around the forehead or the eyes.
  • More sensitivity to light.
  • Pain in the neck, shoulders or back, from the way you hold the device.

Many people notice these symptoms at the end of the day, or after a long session on one device.

Why screens tire your eyes

You blink less

When you focus on a screen, you tend to blink less often and less completely. The surface of the eye then dries out, and the eyes feel gritty or tired.

Your eyes focus near for a long time

Small muscles in the eye work to keep a near object in focus. A phone close to your face makes them work hard, and long sessions give them no rest.

Glare and contrast

A screen that is much brighter or darker than the room, or a screen with reflections, makes your eyes work harder.

Small text and poor posture

Small text makes you lean in and squint. A phone held low makes you bend your neck. Both add to the strain.

Vision problems that are not corrected

If you need glasses, or your glasses are old, screens can make the strain worse. An eye test can find this.

How to relieve digital eye strain

Use the 20-20-20 rule

Every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (about 6 metres) away for at least 20 seconds. This lets the focus muscles in your eyes relax. A window or the far end of a room is a good target.

Blink on purpose

When you take a break, blink slowly and fully a few times. If your eyes still feel dry, ask a pharmacist or an eye doctor about lubricating eye drops.

Adjust your screen

  1. Set the screen brightness close to the light in the room.
  2. Make the text larger, so that you do not lean in.
  3. Hold the phone a comfortable distance from your eyes, and not too low.
  4. Put a computer screen about an arm's length away, with the top at or a little below eye level.
  5. Reduce glare from windows and lamps behind or in front of the screen.

Take real breaks

Short breaks away from all screens help more than a switch from the laptop to the phone. Stand up, walk, drink water and look at things far away.

Reduce unplanned screen time

Long scroll sessions add hours of near focus that you did not plan. Cut those first. See how to reduce screen time on Android and things to do instead of scrolling.

A simple daily routine for your eyes

  1. Before you start screen work, set a timer or a break rule.
  2. Every 20 minutes, look far away for 20 seconds.
  3. Every hour or so, stand up and leave all screens for a few minutes.
  4. After work, choose an activity without a screen for part of the evening.
  5. In the hour before bed, keep screens dim, or put them away.

Children and teenagers can get digital eye strain too. They often do not say that their eyes hurt. Watch for rubbed eyes, squinting or a face very close to the screen. Time outdoors and regular breaks help. See screen addiction in children for more about kids and screens.

What about blue light?

Many people buy blue light glasses for eye strain. Many eye doctors say that the main causes of digital eye strain are less blinking, long near focus and poor setup, not blue light. Breaks and better habits usually help more than special glasses.

Blue light does matter for sleep. Bright screens late at night can delay sleep. A night light mode and less screen use before bed can help. Read phone use before bed and sleep.

This page is general information, not medical advice. See an eye doctor if your symptoms do not go away with rest or if your vision changes. Also see one if you have eye pain, frequent headaches or double vision. Children also need regular eye tests.

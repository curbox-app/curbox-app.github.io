Social media addiction

Social media addiction is compulsive use of social apps that you find hard to control and that harms your mood, sleep, work or relationships. Feeds, likes and endless video make these apps hard to put down. You can cut back without deleting every account.

What is social media addiction?

Social media addiction is a pattern where you feel a strong urge to check social apps and use them longer than you planned. You continue even when it makes you feel worse. It is not a formal diagnosis. It is a common name for a real problem with control.

Social media is not all bad. It helps people keep in touch, find communities and learn new things. The problem is usually not the messages from friends. It is the endless feed, the short videos and the hunt for likes.

Signs of social media addiction

  • You open a social app many times a day without a reason.
  • You lose track of time in feeds or short videos.
  • You check likes and comments again and again after you post.
  • You feel anxious, jealous or low after you scroll.
  • You compare your life with what you see and feel that you fall short.
  • You scroll in bed and sleep later than you want.
  • You tried to take a break but went back within days.

For signs that apply to all phone use, see signs of phone addiction.

Why social media is so addictive

Social rewards

Likes, comments, views and followers measure how people react to you. Humans care a lot about approval from others. These numbers make that approval visible and easy to check.

Endless, personal feeds

Feeds learn what holds your attention and show you more of it. They have no bottom. Short video formats, such as Reels and TikTok, start the next clip on their own. See reels and shorts addiction.

Random rewards

Most posts are average, but some are great. You never know when the next great one will come. This uncertainty keeps you scrolling. See variable rewards and phones.

Fear of missing out

Social apps show what other people do right now. It can feel like you must check often to keep up with friends, trends and news.

Each app has its own pull. For specific guides, see Instagram addiction, TikTok addiction and YouTube addiction.

Social media and mental health

Research on social media and mental health is still developing. The effects seem to differ from person to person and depend on how you use the apps. Passive scrolling and comparison seem to cause more harm for many people than active contact with friends.

Watch how you feel, not only how long you spend. If a certain app or account often leaves you anxious, low or jealous, that is a clear signal to change something. See phone addiction and anxiety and phone addiction and depression.

General information only. This page is not medical advice. If phone use causes serious problems in your life, talk to a doctor or a mental health professional. If you feel unsafe or think about self-harm, contact local crisis support or emergency services now.

How to cut back on social media

You do not need to delete every account. Keep the parts that help you and limit the parts that pull you in.

  1. Check your screen time and find which social app takes the most time.
  2. Turn off notifications for likes, comments and suggestions. Keep direct messages if you need them.
  3. Unfollow or mute accounts that make you feel worse.
  4. Move social apps off the home screen.
  5. Choose set times for social media, such as 20 minutes after dinner.
  6. Put a limit on short videos, which are often the biggest time sink.
  7. Keep phones out of the bedroom.

For more help, see how to break phone addiction and the four-week plan to stop doomscrolling.

How to take a break from social media

A short break can show you how much social media affects your day. It can also make it easier to come back with new rules. Here is a simple one-week break:

  1. Choose one week and tell close friends how to reach you, such as by text or call.
  2. Log out of your social apps, or move them into a folder on the last page of your home screen.
  3. Write down three things that you will do with the free time.
  4. Each evening, note your mood, your sleep and any urges to check.
  5. At the end of the week, decide which apps you want back and with which rules.

The first days are often the hardest. You may feel bored, restless or worried that you miss something. These feelings usually get weaker as the week goes on. For a longer break, see the digital detox guide.

Questions

What is social media addiction?

Social media addiction is compulsive use of social apps that you find hard to control and that harms your mood, sleep, work or relationships. It is not a formal diagnosis, but it describes a real problem with control.

What are the signs of social media addiction?

Common signs are frequent checks without a reason, lost time in feeds, and repeated checks of likes. Other signs are a worse mood after you scroll, comparison with others, and failed attempts to take a break.

Why is social media so addictive?

Social apps offer social rewards such as likes, endless personal feeds, random rewards and a constant fear of missing out. Short video formats start the next clip automatically.

Do I need to delete my social media accounts?

No. Many people do well when they keep messages and limit only the feed or short videos. Set times for social apps and turn off notifications for likes and suggestions.

Is social media bad for mental health?

Research is still developing, and the effects differ from person to person. Passive scrolling and comparison seem to cause more harm for many people than active contact with friends.

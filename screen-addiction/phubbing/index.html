Phubbing: what phone snubbing is and how to stop it

Phubbing is when you look at your phone instead of the person you are with. The word joins "phone" and "snubbing". Most people do it without a plan to be rude, and most people also feel hurt when someone does it to them.

What is phubbing?

Phubbing means to ignore a person who is with you because you pay attention to your phone. It is a short form of "phone snubbing". A person who phubs is a "phubber", and the person who is ignored is "phubbed".

Phubbing can be small: a quick look at a message in the middle of a conversation. It can also be large: a whole dinner where one person scrolls a feed and the other person eats in silence. The size of each act is less important than the pattern. One check is normal. A habit of checks tells the other person that the phone comes first.

Phubbing is not a medical term. It is an everyday word for a common behavior. It often goes together with a wider habit of compulsive phone checking.

Why phubbing hurts relationships

People read attention as a sign of value. When you look away to your phone, the other person can feel less important, even if you only check the time. Research on phubbing suggests that it can lower how close and how satisfied people feel in a conversation.

It breaks the flow of a conversation

Good conversations need small signals: eye contact, nods, quick replies. A phone check stops these signals. The speaker often loses their thought, or decides that the topic is not worth more time.

It spreads

When one person takes out a phone, the other person often does the same. Soon both people scroll, and nobody talks. This pattern is common at family meals and on dates.

It teaches children

Children learn from what adults do. A parent who often looks at a phone in the middle of play or talk shows the child that this is normal. Read more in our guide to parents and phone addiction.

Signs that you phub other people

Phubbing is easy to see in others and hard to see in yourself. Ask yourself these questions honestly:

  • Do you put your phone on the table, screen up, when you eat with someone?
  • Do you check a notification while the other person still talks?
  • Do people often repeat what they said to you?
  • Do you scroll a feed in quiet moments with your partner?
  • Has someone asked you to put your phone away?
  • Do you take your phone out as soon as a conversation slows down?

If you said yes to several, you are not a bad person. You have a habit, and habits can change. For a wider view, try the phone addiction self-check.

How to stop phubbing

The goal is not to hide your phone forever. The goal is to give full attention to people when you are with them. These steps help:

  1. Put the phone out of sight when you sit down with someone. A bag or a pocket is better than the table.
  2. Turn the screen face down if the phone must stay near you.
  3. Agree on phone-free times with the people you live with, such as meals.
  4. Tell the other person before you check something: "I need to answer this message from work."
  5. Answer, then put the phone away again.
  6. Turn off notifications from apps that are not urgent.
  7. Notice the moment when you want to reach for the phone. Ask a question to the other person instead.

What to do if someone phubs you

Talk about it at a calm time, not in the moment of hurt. Describe what you feel, not what the other person is. For example: "When you scroll at dinner, I feel like I talk to myself." Then suggest one small rule that you both follow. If the problem is bigger than one habit, read our page on phone addiction in relationships.

Be fair. Some phone use with other people is fine. You can share a photo, look up a fact together, or check a map. Phubbing is about attention that goes away from the person, not about the phone itself.

Questions

What does phubbing mean?

Phubbing means to ignore a person who is with you because you look at your phone. The word joins phone and snubbing. It can be a quick check in the middle of a conversation or a whole meal spent on a feed.

Is phubbing a sign of phone addiction?

Not always. Many people phub out of habit, not addiction. But sometimes you cannot stop phubbing even when you want to, and it causes problems with people close to you. If this happens often, it can be part of a wider problem with phone use.

How do I tell my partner they phub me?

Pick a calm moment, not the moment you feel ignored. Describe how it makes you feel instead of what your partner is. Then suggest one small shared rule, for example no phones at meals, and follow it yourself too.

Can I use my phone at all when I am with other people?

Yes. Shared use is fine, such as a look at photos together or a check of a map. Phubbing is about attention that moves away from the person. If you need to check something, say so, do it quickly and put the phone away.

News addiction: how to stop doomscrolling the news

News addiction is a habit of checking the news again and again, often bad news, long after you know the main facts. You can stay informed without the constant updates if you choose when, where and how you read the news.

What is news addiction?

News addiction is a strong urge to check headlines, live feeds and news alerts many times a day. It is not a formal diagnosis. When the news is mostly bad and you cannot stop reading, many people call it doomscrolling.

Staying informed is good. You need news to vote, to plan and to understand the world. The problem starts when you check much more often than the news changes, and when the checks leave you anxious instead of informed.

Why bad news is so hard to look away from

Negativity bias

Humans tend to pay more attention to threats and bad news than to good news. Psychologists call this negativity bias. It helped people stay safe in the past. Today, it keeps your eyes on alarming headlines.

The feeling of control

When something frightening happens, more information can feel like safety. You check again to see if anything changed. Usually it did not, and the worry stays.

Live updates and alerts

Live blogs, breaking news alerts and rolling feeds make every hour feel urgent. Most updates add very little, but each one might be important. This uncertainty keeps you checking. See variable rewards and phones.

Feeds that reward outrage

On social media, the posts that make people angry or afraid often spread the fastest. If you read news through a feed, you see more of those posts. See social media addiction.

How news addiction can affect you

  • Mood. Long sessions of bad news can leave you tense, sad or helpless. See phone addiction and anxiety.
  • Sleep. Late news in bed keeps your mind active and can delay sleep.
  • Focus. Frequent checks break your attention at work and at home.
  • Action. Too much news can make you feel that nothing helps, so you do less, not more.
Health note. This page is general information and not medical advice. If the news causes strong anxiety, panic or low mood that does not pass, talk to a doctor or a mental health professional. If you are in crisis, contact local crisis support right away.

How to stop doomscrolling the news

  1. Pick fixed news times. Read the news once or twice a day, for example in the morning and at 6 PM. Outside those times, the news can wait.
  2. Choose a few good sources. Pick two or three trusted sources. Drop the ones that use shock headlines.
  3. Prefer summaries to live feeds. A daily summary, a newsletter or a weekly magazine gives you the facts without the minute by minute stress.
  4. Turn off breaking news alerts. Keep only alerts for your local area or safety, if you need them.
  5. Get news outside social media. Visit the news site directly, not through a feed full of reactions.
  6. No news in bed. Stop the news at least an hour before sleep. See how to stop using your phone in bed.
  7. Turn worry into one action. If a story upsets you, do one useful thing: donate, help a neighbor, contact a representative. Then close the app.

A simple news routine

A good routine is short and fixed. Read one summary in the morning, look at one trusted site in the evening, and read one longer article on the weekend. This gives you the important facts and some depth. It also leaves the rest of the day free from headlines.

Questions

What is news addiction?

News addiction is a strong urge to check headlines, live feeds and news alerts many times a day. It often continues long after you know the main facts. It is not a formal diagnosis. When the news is mostly bad, people often call it doomscrolling.

Why can I not stop reading bad news?

Humans pay more attention to threats than to good news, which is called negativity bias. More information can also feel like control. Live updates and alerts make every hour feel urgent, so you keep checking.

How do I stop doomscrolling the news?

Read the news at one or two fixed times a day, choose a few trusted sources, and prefer daily summaries to live feeds. Turn off breaking news alerts, get news outside social media, and keep the news out of bed.

Is it bad to avoid the news?

A full break for a few days is fine for most people. Over a longer time, it is better to limit the news than to avoid it. Fixed news times let you stay informed with less stress.

Things to do instead of scrolling

The best things to do instead of scrolling give you the same thing the feed gave you. That can be a break, some fun, contact with people or a sense of calm. If you remove the scroll and leave a gap, the gap pulls you back. Here are ideas sorted by what you need and where you are.

First, ask what the scroll gives you

The quickest way to stop scrolling is to replace it with something that meets the same need. Before you pick an idea, ask one question: "What did I want when I opened the app?"

Most answers fall into a few groups. You wanted a rest, some fun, contact with people, information, or relief from a bad feeling. Each need has better answers than an endless feed. This idea comes from the habit loop: keep the cue and the reward, change the routine.

Things to do instead of scrolling, by need

When you need a rest

  • Close your eyes for two minutes and breathe slowly.
  • Look out of a window at something far away. Your eyes will thank you too, see digital eye strain.
  • Make a cup of tea or a glass of water.
  • Stretch your neck, back and hands.
  • Lie on the floor and listen to one song with no screen.

When you need fun

  • Read a few pages of a paper book or an e-reader.
  • Do a crossword, a sudoku or a jigsaw puzzle.
  • Draw, doodle or colour for ten minutes.
  • Play an instrument, even badly.
  • Cook something new.
  • Play a board game or a card game with someone.

When you need people

  • Call a friend instead of reading their posts.
  • Send one message to one person, then close the app.
  • Talk to the people in the room with you.
  • Plan a meeting with a friend for this week.
  • Join a local club, a class or a sports team.

When you need to calm down

  • Go for a short walk, with no destination.
  • Write your thoughts on paper for five minutes.
  • Tidy one small area, such as a desk or a shelf.
  • Water your plants or spend time outside.
  • Take a shower.

When you need something useful

  • Do one small task from your list.
  • Learn ten words in a new language.
  • Listen to a podcast or an audiobook while you walk.
  • Read one long article from start to end, instead of many headlines.

Ideas for the places you scroll most

Many people scroll in the same few places each day. A plan for each place works better than a long general list.

In bed

Keep a book and a lamp next to the bed, and charge the phone in another room. If you cannot sleep, read on paper or listen to calm audio with the screen off. See how to stop using your phone in bed.

In the morning

Do three small things before you look at the phone: drink water, open a window, and stretch. Then decide when you will check messages. Read how to stop checking your phone in the morning.

At work or study

When a task gets hard, the urge to scroll gets strong. Stand up, refill your water, or write the next small step on paper. Short planned breaks help more than random checks. See phone distraction at work.

In the evening

The evening is when many people lose the most time to feeds. Decide on one evening activity before you get home, such as a walk, a call or a meal you cook. Then the free time already has a plan when it arrives.

In a queue or on the bus

Look around you and notice five things. Listen to a podcast with the screen off. Or just wait. Short moments of boredom are normal, and they often give your mind room to think.

How to make the new habit stick

  1. Choose two or three ideas only. A short list is easier to remember.
  2. Put the tools in reach. Keep the book on the sofa and the puzzle on the table.
  3. Link each idea to one cue, for example "when I get into bed, I read".
  4. Make the phone a little harder to reach at those times.
  5. Accept that the new thing feels less exciting at first. That feeling fades.

What to do when an idea does not work

Some ideas will not fit you. That is fine. If you try a book and always put it down after one page, try audio instead. If walks feel boring, call a friend while you walk. Change the idea, not the goal.

Screens are not the enemy. A video call with family, a good film or a game with friends can be a great use of time. The goal is to choose what you do, not to remove every screen.

Questions

What can I do instead of scrolling on my phone?

Pick something that meets the same need. For rest, close your eyes or stretch. For fun, read, draw or do a puzzle. For people, call a friend. For calm, take a short walk or write on paper.

What should I do instead of scrolling in bed?

Keep a book and a lamp next to the bed and charge the phone in another room. If you cannot sleep, read on paper or listen to calm audio with the screen off.

Why do I go back to scrolling even when I have other things to do?

Scrolling is fast, easy and needs no plan. Other activities feel less exciting at first. If the alternative is not in reach at the moment of the urge, the phone usually wins. Put the alternative in reach and link it to a cue.

Is it bad to be bored without my phone?

No. Short moments of boredom are normal and often give your mind room to think. Many people find that boredom feels easier after a few weeks with less scrolling.

Do I have to stop using screens completely?

No. A video call, a good film or a game with friends can be a good use of time. The goal is to choose what you do with your screen time, not to remove every screen.

Study setups you can copy tonight.

Seven ways to use an app blocker for studying on Android, from a quick Pomodoro to a full exam-week lockdown. Pick the one that fits how you study and set it up in a few minutes.

Why your phone wins during study time

Studying is slow and its rewards are far away. Your phone is fast and its rewards are instant. When your attention dips for a second, which it always does on hard material, a notification or the habit of checking Instagram fills the gap. A few of those per hour and a two-hour session quietly becomes forty minutes of work.

An app blocker helps by making the decision once, before you start, instead of fifty times during the session. The setups below all use Curbox, a free, open-source Android app with no ads, no accounts and no internet permission. Each one is a "recipe": what it is good for, what to set, and a tip from how students actually use it.

Set up in 5 minutes, before tonight's session

If you only have time for one thing, do this. It gets you a working study block with no fiddling.

0:00
INSTALL

Get Curbox from the download page and open it. The onboarding asks for three permissions: screen overlay, notifications and the App Blocker accessibility service.

1:30
GROUP

Open the Focus tab, tap Create Focus Group, and name it "Study".

2:30
APPS

Tap Select Apps and pick your usual distractions: social apps, YouTube, games, your browser if it tempts you.

3:30
RULES

Leave "Let me quit mid-sessions" off, turn on "Turn on do not disturb", then Save Group.

4:30
START

Set the slider to 25 minutes, choose "Study" from the dropdown and confirm. Done.

If your phone shows a warning while installing from F-Droid or GitHub, see the setup guide. Deep permissions like accessibility can trigger false alarms.

The recipes

Mix and match. Most students end up with one focus setup for study blocks plus one daily limit that keeps the evenings sane.

Focus ModeStrict

Exam week lockdown

For the week when nothing else matters. Your phone becomes a study tool and nothing more.

Set

  1. Create a Focus Group and choose Block All Except Selected.
  2. Select only what you need: notes, calculator, flashcards, maybe your messaging app for group projects.
  3. Keep "Let me quit mid-sessions" off.
  4. For the time between sessions, add an App Pause group for social apps with a Time Based window (say 8 to 9 PM) and the Never Unlock warning screen, so there is no way past it outside that hour.

An allow-list is stronger than a block list. New distractions you forgot about are blocked automatically.

Focus ModeClassic

Pomodoro 25 / 5

Short, repeatable sprints for when a big task feels impossible to start.

Set

  1. In your Focus Group, turn on Pomodoro timer.
  2. Set the focus time to 25 minutes, the break to 5 minutes, and choose how many rounds, for example 4.
  3. Start it. Curbox plays a sound when each Pomodoro and break starts and ends.

Each Pomodoro counts as one session in Focus Stats. Breaks do not count as focus time, so your numbers stay honest.

NFCRitual

The library desk tag

Turn "sitting down to study" into a single physical action.

Set

  1. Get a cheap writable NFC tag and stick it on your desk, laptop or library card holder.
  2. In Curbox, write a Focus Mode action to it: pick your Study group, a duration, and Start focus (or Toggle focus).
  3. Tap your phone on the tag when you sit down.

Rituals lower the cost of starting. If you set the session as not quittable, the tag cannot end it early either.

Unlock challengePhysical

QR code in another room

When you want a way out, but one that costs you a walk.

Set

  1. Create an App Pause group for your biggest distraction.
  2. Under Configure Warning Screen, choose Require effort to unlock, then QR or Barcode Scan.
  3. Register any code you have, even the barcode on a cereal box, and leave it in the kitchen.

A QR can also grant a set amount of scrolling time, like 10 minutes, so a scan does not mean the rest of the night is gone.

Unlock challengeEarned

Focus first, Instagram later

Social apps stay locked until you have put in the work for the day.

Set

  1. Create an App Pause group with Instagram (and friends).
  2. In Configure Warning Screen, use Link to Focus Mode so there are no unlocks until you have focused for an hour that day.
  3. Do your hour in Focus Mode. After that, the normal unlock opens up.

This turns scrolling into a reward instead of an escape, which tends to feel better than a flat ban.

ReducerBody

Sip & Stretch breaks

For long revision days where you forget to drink water or move for hours.

Set

  1. Turn on Sip & Stretch in Reducers and pick the apps it watches.
  2. Set Break after (for example 50 minutes of use) and Break length (for example 5 minutes).
  3. When the break comes, all apps are blocked until it ends. Stand up, refill your bottle.

Time away from the chosen apps counts as rest, so if you already took a break, the timer starts over.

StatsMotivation

Keep the streak

For people who study better when they can see progress add up.

Set

  1. Tap the bar-chart icon next to the Focus slider to open Focus Stats.
  2. Check total sessions, total focus time, average session length, completion rate and your current day streak.
  3. Use the dropdown to see stats per group, like "Maths" versus "Essays".

Completion rate is the useful one. If it is low, your sessions are probably too long. Shorten them.

Phone or laptop?

On your phone

Everything above runs on Android. Focus Groups can include websites too, added by address or keyword, and they are blocked in supported browsers such as Chrome, Firefox and Brave for the length of the session.

On your laptop

The Curbox browser extension for Chrome and Firefox adds website blocking with schedules and limits, plus a focus mode. Grab it from the download page.

A few things that help more than settings

Put the phone out of reach. Blocking makes distractions harder; distance makes them forgettable. A blocked phone across the room beats a blocked phone in your hand.

Start smaller than you think. A 25-minute session you finish teaches you more than a two-hour session you abandon after twenty minutes.

Batch your notifications. Group chats can still reach you, just not every thirty seconds. Scheduled Notifications holds notifications and delivers them together at times you choose, with calls and alarms still coming through.

Common questions

What is the best way to block apps while studying?

Use a timed focus session for the study block itself, and a scheduled limit for the rest of the day. In Curbox, Focus Mode blocks a saved group of apps and websites until the timer ends, and App Pause handles daily schedules like no social media before 6 PM.

Can I block everything except the apps I need for school?

Yes. When you create a Focus Group, choose the Block All Except Selected mode and pick only what you need, such as your notes app, calculator, dictionary or a flashcard app. Everything else is blocked for the session.

Does Curbox have a Pomodoro timer?

Yes. Turn on the Pomodoro timer option in a Focus Group. It repeats your chosen focus time for a set number of rounds with a short break of your choosing between each, and plays a sound when a session, Pomodoro or break starts and ends.

Can I stop myself from ending a focus session early?

Yes. Leave the "Let me quit mid-sessions" option off when you create the Focus Group. The session then runs until the timer ends, and a session marked as not quittable cannot be ended early with an NFC tag either.

Is Curbox free for students?

Curbox is free for everyone, with no ads, accounts or subscription. It is open source under the GPL v3 and the Android app has no internet permission.

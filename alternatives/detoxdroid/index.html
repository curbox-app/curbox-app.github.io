DetoxDroid alternative: two open-source blockers, side by side.

DetoxDroid is one of the best free, open-source digital detox apps on Android, and we mean that. If you are searching for a DetoxDroid alternative, it is probably because you want something it does not do, not because it does its job badly. Curbox and DetoxDroid share a license, an offline design, and a lot of goals. This page shows where they differ so you can pick the right one, or run both.

What DetoxDroid does well

DetoxDroid's headline idea is to make your phone less colorful and less sticky by default. Its overlay turns apps gray, dims, and on Android 12 and newer blurs them, "from the minute you install DetoxDroid. No computer, no root, nothing to grant." For true system grayscale and an Extra Dim below normal minimum brightness, it needs the WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS permission, granted over adb from a computer, through Shizuku, or with root.

Its doomscroll breaker detects long scrolling sessions, interrupts them, and locks that feed for a cooldown, and it counts the short videos you swipe through on Reels, Shorts, and TikTok. Emergency Brake switches every feature to its strictest setting, with a ten-minute wait to end early. Apps can be delayed, put behind a warning screen, or deactivated outright, including their icon and notifications. Daily time budgets can be set per day or per clock hour, websites can be blocked by category in Chrome, Firefox, and most browsers built on them, and a commitment password locks chosen settings. It also has usage stats with widgets, Do Not Disturb while it runs, a pause button, and a minimal text launcher widget.

That is a strong feature set from a fellow open-source project, and it is free with no paid tier.

Grayscale: the honest comparison

Grayscale is DetoxDroid's signature feature, so it deserves a careful answer. Neither app can turn on Android's real grayscale with a simple permission dialog, because the setting needs special access.

DetoxDroid

  • Works out of the box as an overlay that paints over the screen
  • Real grayscale and Extra Dim need adb from a computer, Shizuku, or root
  • You can exclude apps such as the camera or gallery

Curbox

  • Grayscale Filter turns chosen apps gray, leaving the rest in color
  • Needs the free Shizuku helper
  • On Android 11 and newer, Shizuku can start through Wireless Debugging, no computer needed

If you want grayscale with zero setup, DetoxDroid's overlay is the easier start. If you already use Shizuku, both reach the real thing. Our per-app grayscale guide covers every route, including Android's built-in options.

Moving your DetoxDroid setup to Curbox

  1. Daily budgets. Reducers → Apps → + → Usage Based, set minutes per day. Turn off the uniform setting to give weekdays and weekends different limits.
  2. Doomscroll breaker. Reducers → Short-Form Video Content, then pick Time Based or Reel Count Based. Limits are shared across Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, and Facebook Reels. Curbox does not list TikTok, so block TikTok as a whole app with App Pause.
  3. Warning screen and delays. In any group, Configure Warning Screen → Wait to unlock, and add the delay timer. For a harder lock, choose Never Unlock.
  4. Commitment password. There is no settings password. The closest options are adding Curbox itself to an app blocking group, an effort challenge such as a QR code kept in another room, and anti-uninstall protection in the F-Droid variant.
  5. Websites. Reducers → Keywords And Websites → +. There are no built-in category lists, but you can import a .txt list, and the community Discord shares lists. See the website blocking guide.
  6. Grayscale. Reducers → Grayscale Filter → Grant Permission on the Shizuku card → + and choose apps.

Running both

Because both are offline and open source, some people use DetoxDroid for its gray overlay and Curbox for limits and unlock challenges. Both rely on Android's Accessibility Service and draw over other apps, so give each tool a separate job, test that their screens do not fight, and turn off duplicate features such as Do Not Disturb in one of them. We have not tested every combination, so start small.

When DetoxDroid is the better pick

Choose DetoxDroid if grayscale, dimming, and blur are what help you most, if you need TikTok covered by a doomscroll limit, if you want a password on your settings, or if you like the idea of everything going strict at once with Emergency Brake. Choose Curbox if you want unlock challenges that make you get up, count-based limits on short videos, focus sessions with Pomodoro, batched notifications, feed hiding inside apps, or the same tool in your desktop browser.

DetoxDroid alternative FAQ

Is DetoxDroid open source?

Yes. DetoxDroid is licensed GPL-3.0-only, with source on GitHub, and it is distributed on F-Droid. Curbox is also GPL v3.

Does DetoxDroid need a computer for grayscale?

Not for its overlay, which works right after install. For true system grayscale and Extra Dim it needs a special permission, granted over adb from a computer, through Shizuku, or with root. Curbox's Grayscale Filter also needs Shizuku, which on Android 11 and newer can run without a computer.

Does Curbox block TikTok short videos?

Curbox's short-form video limits and counter support Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, and Facebook Reels. TikTok is not on that list, so block or limit the TikTok app as a whole with App Pause.

Can I use DetoxDroid and Curbox together?

Yes, both are free and offline. Give each a separate job, for example DetoxDroid for grayscale and Curbox for limits and unlock challenges, and switch off features that overlap.

Do either of them connect to the internet?

The Curbox Android app has no internet permission in any variant. DetoxDroid's F-Droid permission list does not include internet access either.

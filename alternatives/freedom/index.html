A Freedom app alternative built for the phone.

Freedom is one of the best-known blockers, and its biggest strength is reach: one account blocks apps and websites across your computer, phone and tablet at once. People look for a Freedom app alternative on Android when they want more control on the phone itself, such as Reels limits, daily allowances and unlocks that take effort, or when they would rather not pay a subscription for scheduling and Locked Mode.

Two different ideas of blocking

Freedom and Curbox overlap, but they start from different places. Knowing which one matches your problem saves you a lot of fiddling.

Freedom

Sessions that wall off time

You start a session, or schedule one, and the things on your blocklist are unavailable everywhere until it ends. It works well for deep work blocks on a computer: 9 to 12, no news, no social, no email.

On the free plan, sessions last up to two hours. Recurring schedules, longer sessions and Locked Mode, which stops you quitting early, are Premium features on Freedom's pricing page.

Curbox

Rules that shape the day

Curbox has sessions too (Focus Mode), but most of its tools are standing rules: Instagram only after 6 PM, YouTube Shorts capped at 20 videos, a pause screen every time you open a game. They run all day without you starting anything.

When you hit a rule you choose what happens: no way through, a wait, or an effort task such as scanning a QR code, typing a sentence or stating your intent. All of it is free.

Moving your Freedom setup to Curbox

These are the Freedom habits people most often rebuild, with the path in Curbox for each.

  1. Your blocklist

    Make a Focus Group with the same apps and sites. Websites are added as addresses or keywords, so youtube.com/shorts blocks only Shorts and leaves the rest of YouTube.

    Focus tab → Create Focus Group → Select Apps → Add Websites

  2. A session you start by hand

    Set the timer with the slider, choose your group and confirm. Turn on Pomodoro timer in the group if you work in rounds with breaks.

    Focus tab → slider → choose group → confirm

  3. Recurring sessions

    For a block that repeats every day, use App Pause instead of Focus Mode. A Time Based group allows apps only in the hours you choose, every day or only on chosen days.

    Reducers → Apps → + → Time Based → Configure Allowed Schedule

  4. Locked Mode

    In the Focus Group, leave Let me quit mid-sessions off. For App Pause groups, set the warning screen to Never Unlock. The F-Droid variant adds anti-uninstall protection on top.

    Configure Warning Screen → Unlock Challenge → Never Unlock

  5. Blocking the whole internet

    Curbox does not cut off the network. The closest setup is a Focus Group in Block All Except Selected mode that leaves only the apps you need, like Phone, Messages and Maps.

    Create Focus Group → block mode → Block All Except Selected

  6. Your laptop

    Install the Curbox browser extension for website blocking, limits, schedules and focus mode in the browser. Optional Sync can line up focus mode between the extension and your phone through the Curbox Sync companion app.

    Downloads → Browser extension

What Curbox adds on Android

  • Limits inside apps. Cap Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts by minutes or video count while messages, search and subscriptions keep working.
  • Daily allowances. Usage Based groups give an app a set number of minutes a day, with different limits on different weekdays if you want.
  • Friction you choose. QR or barcode scans, NFC tags, adaptive math, typing a sentence, stating your intent, or a fixed or unpredictable wait.
  • Nudges that are not blocks. Grayscale for chosen apps, a live Reels counter, a personal message over an app, scheduled notification delivery.
  • No account and no internet permission. The Android app works fully offline. Freedom needs an account, since syncing across devices is the point of it.

Using both is reasonable. Some people keep Freedom for their computer, where it blocks desktop apps that Curbox cannot touch, and use Curbox on their Android phone for feed limits and unlock challenges. The two do not conflict, though each will need its own permissions on the phone if you install both there.

When Freedom is the better pick

  • You want one blocklist on Mac, Windows, iPhone, Android and Chromebook, started from any of them.
  • Your distractions are mostly desktop apps, such as a game client or a chat app on your computer.
  • You like blocking the entire internet for a fixed stretch.
  • Two-hour sessions on the free plan are enough, or you are happy with Premium.

Freedom alternative FAQ

Is Freedom available on Android?

Yes. Freedom blocks apps and websites on Android, and the same account works on Mac, Windows, iPhone, iPad and Chromebook.

Is there a free alternative to Freedom for Android?

Curbox is free on Android with no paid tier. It has focus sessions, recurring schedules, daily limits and a mode where sessions cannot be ended early. It does not cover iPhone or desktop apps.

Can Curbox block the whole internet like Freedom?

No. Curbox does not switch off the network. The nearest setup is a Focus Group in Block All Except Selected mode, which blocks every app except the few you choose.

What is the Curbox version of Locked Mode?

Leave "Let me quit mid-sessions" off in a Focus Group, or set an App Pause group's unlock challenge to Never Unlock. The F-Droid variant can add anti-uninstall protection.

Does Curbox sync between my phone and computer?

Optionally. The Curbox browser extension can pair with the Curbox Sync companion app on your phone so focus mode and screen time line up. Data goes straight to your phone, not to a Curbox server.

Can I keep Freedom on my laptop and use Curbox on my phone?

Yes. Many people do exactly that: Freedom for desktop apps on the computer, Curbox for Reels limits and unlock challenges on Android.

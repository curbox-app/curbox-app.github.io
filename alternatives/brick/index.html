Alternatives · Brick

A Brick alternative for Android, built from a sticker.

Brick is a small physical block: tap your phone on it and your distracting apps lock until you come back and tap again. If you want that on Android without buying the device, the closest Brick alternative is a writable NFC tag or a QR code paired with Curbox, a free, open-source app blocker. This page shows how to build it, and is straight about what a sticker cannot copy.

What Brick actually is

Brick is made by Brick LLC. The device costs $59 on Brick's own store, and the company also sells Brick Night, a $129 Bluetooth version meant for the bedroom. Its product pages list no subscription. You choose apps in the Brick app, group them into modes (up to 10), then tap your phone to the device to start blocking. Unblocking needs another tap on the same device.

It works on Android 12 or newer and on iPhone with iOS 18 or newer. On Android it can block any app except Phone and the launcher, it uses the Accessibility Service to do so, and its Play listing describes the hardware as NFC powered. Schedules can start Brick for you, and you can even start a session remotely, but ending one still needs the device. A Strict Mode stops you from deleting the app or changing settings to escape. If the Brick goes missing, the app gives you five Emergency Unbricks, and you can email the company for more.

The idea works because the key lives somewhere other than your hand. That part costs nothing to copy.

Pick your route

Curbox can copy Brick's tap in two ways. They feel different, so read both before you build.

Route A · Tap to start a session

Write a Focus Mode action to an NFC tag. Tapping it starts a timed session that blocks a Focus Group of apps and websites. You can set the tag to Start, Stop, or Toggle.

Closest to the Brick ritual. The catch: a session marked as not quittable cannot be ended with a tag either, so you trade strictness for a tap-to-stop.

Route B · Tap to unlock each app

Set an app limit, then make the pause screen's Unlock Challenge an NFC tag or a QR scan. The app stays blocked until you physically fetch the key.

Stricter, and it works on a schedule with no tap needed to start. It is the true "walk across the room" version.

Assembly instructions

  1. Install Curbox and finish onboarding

    Get it from the downloads page. Onboarding asks for the overlay, notification, and App Blocker accessibility permissions. Setup takes under five minutes.

  2. Make the list of things to block

    For Route A, open the Focus tab and tap Create Focus Group. Name it, pick apps, add websites if you want, and choose Block Selected or Block All Except Selected. The second option keeps only the apps you choose, much like a Brick mode that leaves calls, texts, and email.

    For Route B, go to Reducers → Apps, tap +, select apps, and pick a mode: Time Based to block outside certain hours, Usage Based for a daily cap, or On Each Open to block every launch.

  3. Attach the key

    Route A: write a Focus Mode action to your tag: pick the Focus Group, a duration, and Start, Stop, or Toggle. Route B: in the group, tap Configure Warning Screen, choose Require effort to unlock, then NFC Tag or QR or Barcode Scan, and register your tag or code.

    No NFC on your phone? A QR code or the barcode on a cereal box works the same way, and a QR can also grant a fixed amount of scrolling time. More in the QR unlock guide and the NFC guide.

  4. Close the obvious escape hatches

    Add Curbox itself to an app blocking group so settings are not one tap away. Use Proceed Limits to cap how many times you can get through in a window. In the F-Droid variant, turn on anti-uninstall protection, which uses device administrator access. Turning that off later means deactivating device admin in Android settings, and on some phones that needs a restart into safe mode first.

  5. Decide what happens if you lose the key

    Brick gives you emergency unbricks. With a sticker, you write the fallback yourself, so do it now while you are calm. Some options: register a barcode on something you can always buy again, keep a timed Wait to unlock on less critical groups instead of a tag, or choose a Focus session length you can live with if the tag vanishes.

    Do not pair Never Unlock with anything you might need in an emergency. A blocker should hold back an impulse, not your bank app.

  6. Put the key somewhere annoying

    The whole trick is distance. A tag stuck next to your bed defeats the point.

What a sticker cannot copy

It would be dishonest to call a cheap sticker the same product. Brick is a better pick if these matter to you:

01

It is an object with a job. A weighty block on your desk is a visible commitment. A sticker inside a cupboard is easy to forget, which cuts both ways.

02

It works on iPhone. Curbox is Android plus a browser extension. If your household mixes phones, Brick covers both.

03

It is a finished product. Pairing, modes, emergency unbricks, and Brick Night for the bedroom are designed to work together. The DIY route asks you to make a few decisions yourself.

04

Strict Mode on every phone. Curbox's anti-uninstall protection is only in the F-Droid variant; the Playstore variant relies on blocking Curbox's own settings.

What you get in return: no hardware to buy, no account, nothing leaving your phone, code anyone can read, and finer tools than whole-app blocking. Curbox can limit just Reels, just Shorts, or hide the Instagram feed while messages keep working.

Brick alternative FAQ

Does Brick work on Android?

Yes. Brick's FAQ says it works with Android 12 or later and with iOS 18 or later, and the Brick app is on Google Play.

Can a cheap NFC sticker replace a Brick?

For the core idea, yes: Curbox can require a registered NFC tag to unlock an app, or start and stop Focus Mode when you tap a tag. What you give up is the finished product: the device itself, emergency unbricks, iPhone support, and Brick Night.

Do I need NFC on my phone?

Only for the tag route. Curbox's QR or Barcode Scan challenge does the same job with the camera, and you can register a code you already have, such as the barcode on a product box.

Is Brick a subscription?

Brick's product pages show a one-time $59 price for the device and do not list a subscription. Curbox is free with no paid tier.

Can I end a Focus session early with my NFC tag?

Only if the session allows quitting. A session marked as not quittable cannot be ended early with a tag either. If you want the tag to be the only way out, use Route B and make the tag the unlock challenge for the app group.

Is Curbox open source?

Yes. Curbox is licensed under GPL v3 and the full source is on GitHub. The Android app has no internet permission, so your blocking rules and usage stay on your phone.

Alternatives · Digital Wellbeing

When the timer runs out and you just delete it.

Digital Wellbeing is a good first step, and it costs nothing. Many people search for an alternative after the same moment: the timer ends, and getting past it is as easy as setting it was. Here is what Digital Wellbeing actually does, where it stops, and how to rebuild your setup in Curbox with limits that hold.

What Digital Wellbeing gives you

Digital Wellbeing ships with many Android phones and lives in your Settings app. Google's help page says it is available "on some phones," and the exact steps vary by device. It has four main tools for cutting back:

App timers

Settings › Digital Wellbeing › App timers

A daily time limit per app. When time runs out, the app closes and its icon dims. Timers reset at midnight.

Site timers

Dashboard › Chrome › Set site timer

A daily limit for a website, but only for sites you visit in Chrome.

Focus mode

Digital Wellbeing › Focus mode

Pauses the apps you pick and silences their notifications. It can run on a schedule, and "Take a break" unpauses apps for a time you choose.

Bedtime mode

Digital Wellbeing › Bedtime mode

On a schedule or while charging, it can turn the whole screen grayscale, dim the wallpaper, and filter notifications.

For a lot of people this is enough. Seeing a dimmed icon is a real nudge, and nothing needs installing.

Where it stops working for many people

The limit is only as strong as the menu. Google's help page gives one way to use an app again after its timer runs out: go back to App timers and delete the timer. There is no wait, no question, and no way to make that step harder for yourself, apart from a PIN option meant for setting limits on a device someone else uses. If the urge is strong at 11 PM, it wins in a few taps.

It works on whole apps, not the part that hooks you. You can limit YouTube, but not YouTube Shorts on their own. You can limit Instagram, but then you lose messages along with Reels.

Grayscale is all or nothing. Bedtime mode turns the entire screen gray during its schedule. You cannot make only Instagram gray at 3 PM while your camera and maps keep their color.

Website limits only cover Chrome. Site timers live inside Digital Wellbeing's Chrome view. Switch to Firefox or Brave and the limit does not follow.

"Take a break" is built in. Focus mode includes a one-tap way to unpause apps for a while. That is kind, and sometimes too kind.

Feature by feature

  • App timers become App Pause in Usage Based mode. You can set different limits per weekday, such as 15 minutes on weekdays and 60 on weekends, or 0 on Sunday.
  • Site timers become the Keywords and Websites reducer. It can block a whole site (youtube.com), one section (youtube.com/shorts), or a path on every site (/reels). It works in Chrome, Brave, Firefox, Fennec, Opera, Cromite, Vanadium, Helium, CalyxOS Chromium, /e/OS Browser, and Waterfox. Other browsers can be blocked as apps.
  • Focus mode becomes Curbox Focus Mode, with saved Focus Groups, an allow-list mode ("Block All Except Selected"), a Pomodoro timer, and Focus Stats with streaks. You decide whether quitting early is allowed at all.
  • Bedtime grayscale becomes the Grayscale Filter, applied per app. It needs the free Shizuku helper. Bedtime silence becomes Auto DND on a schedule.
  • The dimmed icon becomes the pause screen, and that is the biggest change. You choose what it takes to get past it: nothing, a wait, a QR or barcode scan, an NFC tag, a typed sentence, adaptive math, or no way through at all.

When Digital Wellbeing is the better pick

You want nothing extra on your phone. It is already there. Curbox, like every Android app blocker, needs the Accessibility Service and permission to draw over other apps. Digital Wellbeing is part of the system and needs neither.
You are setting limits for a child. Digital Wellbeing ties into Google Family Link, where app limits and bedtime for a supervised account are controlled from the parent's side. Curbox is built for adults limiting themselves.
A dimmed icon already works for you. If you have never deleted a timer in a weak moment, you do not need more friction. Stay with what is built in.

Rebuild your setup in Curbox

Setup takes less than five minutes. Grant the three core permissions during onboarding (Screen Overlay, Notifications, and the App Blocker accessibility service), then recreate whatever you had. You can leave your Digital Wellbeing timers in place while you test; delete them once Curbox is doing the job, so you do not get two different screens for one app.

1. A daily app timer that holds

Replaces: App timers

  1. Open Reducers › Apps and tap +.
  2. Name the group, for example "Social", and tap Select Apps.
  3. Choose Usage Based and set the daily limit. Turn off the uniform setting if weekends should differ.
  4. Tap Configure Warning Screen. Pick Require effort to unlock, then QR or Barcode Scan, and register any code that lives in another room, like a cereal box.
  5. Add a 10 second delay timer and a Proceed Limit, then tap Done.

2. Keep YouTube, lose Shorts

Replaces: limiting all of YouTube

  1. Open Reducers › Short-Form Video Content and turn on the main switch.
  2. Choose Reel Count Based and set a count, or Time Based for minutes.
  3. The limit is shared across YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, and Facebook Reels, so you cannot dodge it by switching apps.
  4. For Shorts in a browser, add youtube.com/shorts under Reducers › Keywords And Websites.

3. Focus without an easy break button

Replaces: Focus mode

  1. On the Focus tab, tap Create Focus Group and name it "Work".
  2. Select Apps, and add websites if you like.
  3. Pick Block Selected, or Block All Except Selected if you only need two or three apps while you work.
  4. Leave Let me quit mid-sessions off. Turn on Turn on do not disturb and the Pomodoro timer if you want breaks.
  5. Set the slider to 25 minutes and start.

4. Gray only the apps that pull you in

Replaces: Bedtime mode grayscale

  1. Install Shizuku and start it. On Android 11 and up, Wireless Debugging works without a computer.
  2. In Curbox, open Reducers › Grayscale Filter and tap Grant Permission.
  3. Tap +, name the group, select apps, and tap Done.
  4. For bedtime quiet, add a schedule under Reducers › Auto DND. A range cannot cross midnight, so split 10 PM to 7 AM into two ranges.

Questions people ask

Can I use Curbox and Digital Wellbeing at the same time?

Yes. They do not conflict. Many people keep Digital Wellbeing's dashboard and Bedtime mode and move app limits to Curbox. Avoid limiting the same app in both, or you will see two different block screens.

Is Curbox harder to bypass than Digital Wellbeing?

It can be, if you set it up that way. Curbox lets you choose the effort needed to get past a limit, from a short wait to scanning a code in another room, or no way past at all. You can also cap how many times you can continue. The F-Droid and GitHub builds add anti-uninstall protection; the Play Store build does not include it.

Does Curbox cost anything or show ads?

No. Curbox is free, open source under the GPL v3, has no ads, and needs no account. The Android app has no internet permission, so your usage data stays on your phone.

Can Curbox block a website in Firefox or Brave?

Yes. The Keywords and Websites reducer works in Chrome, Brave, Firefox, Fennec, Opera, Cromite, Vanadium, Helium, CalyxOS Chromium, /e/OS Browser, and Waterfox. For any other browser, block the browser itself with App Pause.

Will I lose my usage history from Digital Wellbeing?

Digital Wellbeing keeps its own history, and Curbox does not import it. Curbox starts tracking from the moment you grant its permissions, with a daily total, a weekly chart, per-app and per-website stats, and CSV export.

Does Curbox work for parental controls?

Curbox is designed for people limiting their own use. For supervising a child's phone, Google Family Link with Digital Wellbeing is the better tool.

Try it on one app first

Pick the app whose timer you delete most often. Put it in a Usage Based group with a QR scan to unlock, and live with it for a week. If that is all you ever use Curbox for, that is fine.

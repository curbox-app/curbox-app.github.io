Alternatives · Opal

An Opal alternative for Android that costs nothing.

Opal is a polished screen time app, and since its Android release you can use it on a Pixel or a Galaxy as well as an iPhone. If you are searching for an Opal alternative on Android, it is usually because of the price of Pro, or because you want Hard Mode and Allow Only style blocking without a subscription. This page shows what each Opal rule becomes in Curbox, and when Opal is still the better buy.

What Opal costs over three years

Opal has a free plan that never expires, with three rules and basic app blocking. The features most people sign up for, such as unlimited rules, Hard Mode and Allow Only, are part of Opal Pro. These are the US prices listed on Opal's pricing page in November 2026. On Android, Opal says prices depend on your country and show in Google Play before you pay.

Rebuild your Opal rules in Curbox

Opal organises blocking around rules: Schedules, App Limits and Open Limits, plus Focus Sessions you start by hand. Curbox has a counterpart for each. Every card below is a real setup path in the Curbox app.

Opal rule
Schedule

In Curbox this is an App Pause group in Time Based mode, which allows apps only inside a time window.

  1. Reducers → Apps → tap +
  2. Name it and Select Apps
  3. Choose Time Based, then Configure Allowed Schedule
Note: one window cannot cross midnight. Split 11 PM to 2 AM into two.
Opal rule
App Limit

An App Pause group in Usage Based mode. It blocks once you reach a daily time cap, and the counter resets at midnight.

  1. Reducers → Apps → +
  2. Choose Usage Based and set the minutes
  3. Optional: turn off the uniform setting to give weekdays and weekends different limits
Bonus: stack it with a Time Based group on the same app.
Opal rule
Open Limit

Use On Each Open mode, so the pause screen appears every launch. Then cap how often you can get through with Proceed Limits.

  1. Create a group in On Each Open mode
  2. Configure Warning Screen → set a Proceed Limit, for example 5 times a day
Close match: it limits passes through the block, not raw opens.
Opal session
Focus Session

Curbox Focus Mode. Pick a length on the slider, pick a Focus Group, and start. A Pomodoro option repeats rounds with breaks.

  1. Focus tab → Create Focus Group
  2. Add apps and, if you like, websites
  3. Set the timer and confirm
Stats: Focus Stats tracks sessions, completion rate and streaks.

Hard Mode and Allow Only, without Pro

Opal Hard Mode → Curbox Never Unlock

Opal describes Hard Mode as having no way out of a session. In Curbox, the matching tools are free. For Focus Mode, turn off Let me quit mid-sessions in the Focus Group. For App Pause groups, set the warning screen's unlock challenge to Never Unlock. If you want something in between, pick an effort challenge such as a QR code kept in another room, or adaptive math.

The F-Droid variant also has anti-uninstall protection, so removing Curbox in a weak moment takes real effort. The Playstore variant does not include it.

Opal Allow Only → Block All Except Selected

Opal Pro's Allow Only blocks everything except chosen apps. In Curbox, open the Focus tab, create a Focus Group, and pick the block mode Block All Except Selected. Add the apps you need during the session, such as Phone, Messages, Maps and your music app. Everything else waits until the timer ends. The allowlist guide has ready-made lists.

What you give up by leaving Opal

Curbox is a different kind of project, and some of Opal's strengths do not carry over.

  • iPhone and Mac. Opal runs on iPhone, Android and Mac with one account. Curbox is Android only, plus a browser extension for Chrome and Firefox. There is no iOS version yet.
  • Opal Score and weekly reports. Opal wraps your week into a single score and a report. Curbox shows the raw numbers instead: daily and weekly usage, a per-app list, a usage heatmap and Focus Stats, with CSV export.
  • Gems and streak rewards. Opal awards milestone badges. Curbox has a focus day streak but no badge system.
  • Polish and support. Opal is a company with a help centre and a team. Curbox is maintained in the open with help from its community and a Discord.

In return you get things Opal does not offer: limits on Reels and Shorts inside the apps, effort-based unlocks such as QR codes and NFC tags, website blocking in many Android browsers, an app with no internet permission, and source code anyone can read.

When Opal is the better pick

Stay with Opal if any of these describe you:

  • You use an iPhone, or switch between iPhone, Android and Mac and want one setup on all of them.
  • You like having one score that sums up your week, and the badges keep you going.
  • The free plan's three rules already cover what you need.
  • You already bought the lifetime plan and it works for you.

Opal alternative FAQ

Is Opal available on Android?

Yes. Opal runs on iPhone, Android and Mac. Its Android app is on Google Play, and Opal says Android prices depend on your country.

Is there a free Opal alternative for Android?

Curbox is free with no paid tier. It covers Opal-style schedules, app limits, open limits, focus sessions and an allow-only mode, and adds Reels and Shorts limits and effort-based unlocks. It is open source under the GPL v3.

What replaces Opal Hard Mode in Curbox?

Turn off "Let me quit mid-sessions" in a Focus Group so a session cannot be ended early, or set an App Pause group's unlock challenge to Never Unlock. The F-Droid variant can also add anti-uninstall protection.

Can Curbox show an Opal Score?

No. Curbox does not turn your week into a single score. It shows daily and weekly screen time, per-app usage, a heatmap and Focus Stats, and you can export the data to CSV.

Do I need an account to use Curbox?

No. The Android app has no accounts and no internet permission. Your rules and usage history stay on your phone.

Does Curbox work on iPhone like Opal?

Not yet. Curbox is Android only, plus a browser extension for Chrome and Firefox. If you need iPhone support, Opal is the better choice for now.

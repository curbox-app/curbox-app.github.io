Guide · Notifications

Get your notifications three times a day, not three hundred.

Every buzz is an invitation to pick up your phone, and most of them can wait. Here is what Android can do on its own, where it falls short, and how to batch notifications into a few deliveries a day.

Why batching helps

One notification is harmless. The problem is the pattern: the phone lights up, you check it, and while you are there you open something else. Switching your attention back to what you were doing takes time, and dozens of small interruptions a day add up to a lot of it.

Batching keeps the information and drops the interruptions. You still see every message, just at times you chose, when you were going to look at your phone anyway. It is the same idea as checking email at set times instead of keeping the inbox open all day.

What Android can do on its own

Stock Android has several tools for quieter notifications. They are all useful, but none of them collects notifications and hands them back together on a schedule. Menu names below are for Pixel and near-stock phones; other brands have similar settings in slightly different places.

Set up scheduled delivery in Curbox

  1. Install Curbox from the downloads page and finish the short setup.
  2. Go to Reducers › Scheduled Notifications.
  3. Turn on Enable Scheduled Notifications. Curbox asks for notification access; this is the only extra permission the feature needs.
  4. Under Delivery times, keep the default 9 AM, 1 PM and 6 PM, or add and remove times. You can have up to 12.
  5. Under Apps that can always notify, add anything that truly cannot wait, such as a work chat on call days or a family messenger.

At each delivery time, a single Curbox notification tells you how many notifications were waiting, and they reappear in your shade.

What notification access is used for

Curbox holds notifications by snoozing them until your next delivery time, which means Android itself keeps them safe and nothing is lost even if Curbox is closed. It does not read, save or send notification content anywhere. The Android app has no internet permission in any variant, so it could not send them even if it wanted to. If you switch the feature off, anything already held still arrives at the next delivery time. More in the privacy policy.

Pair it with Auto DND

Scheduled Notifications decides when notifications arrive. Auto DND decides when your phone stays silent altogether, for example overnight or during a weekly meeting. Set it up in Reducers › Auto DND › +, name the group, choose a time schedule and tap Done. One schedule cannot cross midnight, so for 11 PM to 7 AM create two: 11 PM to 12 AM and 12 AM to 7 AM.

Questions

Does Android have a scheduled notification summary like iPhone?

Not in stock Android. Do Not Disturb, snoozing, per-app channels and notification cooldown can quiet or delay notifications, but none of them collects notifications and hands them back together at set times. You need an app for that, such as Curbox's Scheduled Notifications.

Will I miss calls or alarms if I batch notifications?

Not with Curbox. Calls, alarms and ongoing notifications such as music or navigation always come through right away, as do any apps you add to the always-notify list.

Can Curbox read my notifications?

Curbox needs notification access to hold notifications, which it does by snoozing them until your next delivery time. It does not read, save or send notification content anywhere, and the Android app has no internet permission at all.

What happens to held notifications if I turn the feature off?

They are not lost. Anything already held still comes back at the next delivery time.

How many delivery times can I set?

Up to 12 a day. The default is three: 9 AM, 1 PM and 6 PM.

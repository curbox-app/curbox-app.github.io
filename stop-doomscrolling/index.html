Guide · Stop doomscrolling

Stop doomscrolling in four weeks, one small step at a time.

You don't need a phone detox or a heroic act of willpower. You need to see the habit, slow it down, fence it in, and then give your hands something better to do. Here is a plan for each of those, one per week.

Why the scroll is so sticky

Doomscrolling is rarely a decision. You pick up your phone to check one message, and twenty minutes later you are deep in a feed you did not mean to open. That is not a character flaw. Feeds are built to have no bottom, and every swipe might bring something funny, outrageous or new. Rewards that arrive unpredictably are some of the hardest habits to drop, because the next one always feels close.

So the plan below does not ask you to try harder. It changes the setup around you, so the easy path and the path you actually want are the same one. Each week adds a single layer on top of the last.

No stopping pointAn endless feed never tells you that you are done. You have to supply that signal yourself.
AutopilotYour thumb opens the app before you have consciously decided anything.
Lost timeTwenty minutes in a feed feels like five. Without a number, you cannot tell.
Nothing to replace itRemove the scroll and leave a gap, and the gap pulls you straight back.

The four-week plan

Every step uses Curbox on Android, which is free, open source and has no internet permission. If a week feels hard, stay on it longer. Moving on before a step feels normal is the most common reason plans like this fall apart.

  1. 01 Week 1 · Awareness

    Just watch. Don't block anything yet.

    The first week is about honest numbers. Most people underestimate how much they scroll, and it is hard to change something you only have a vague feeling about. Seeing the real total is often enough to make you put the phone down a little sooner, with no rules at all.

    Put the numbers somewhere you will see them without trying. A widget on your home screen does this far better than an app you have to remember to open.

    Set up this week

    1. Screentime Widget. In Curbox, open the Usage tab, tap the three-dot menu, choose Pin Widget, then the Screentime Widget. Your total for today now sits on your home screen.
    2. Short-Form Video Counter. Go to Reducers, then Short-Form Video Counter, and switch it on. A live number appears while you scroll Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts or Facebook Reels. Quick swipes past a video are not counted.
    3. Look at the heatmap. After a few days, check the usage heatmap in your stats. It shows which hours and days you reach for the phone most. Late night and the first minutes after waking are common hotspots.

    You are ready for week 2 when you can roughly guess your daily total before you look at it.

  2. 02 Week 2 · Friction

    Put a speed bump between the urge and the feed.

    Now that you know where your time goes, slow down the moment of opening. You are not saying no yet. You are giving the thinking part of your brain a few seconds to catch up with your thumb. Even a short pause breaks the autopilot more often than you would expect.

    Set up this week

    1. A delay timer. Create an App Pause group (Reducers, then Apps, then the + button) for your worst app with the On Each Open mode, and under Configure Warning Screen choose Wait to unlock with a short fixed time. During the countdown the screen brightens and the phone vibrates, a small physical reminder that you are making a choice.
    2. A message to yourself. Go to Reducers, then Gentle Prompts, and enable Mindful Messages for the same apps. Write something honest, and add live placeholders. For example: {reel_count} reels today. Still worth it? or {app_usage_today} in here already. The numbers update every time the message appears.
    3. Grayscale. Colour is a big part of why feeds feel rewarding. The Grayscale Filter turns chosen apps black and white while the rest of your phone stays in colour. It needs the free Shizuku helper; the Grayscale Filter card in Reducers walks you through granting it.

    You are ready for week 3 when you notice yourself closing an app at the pause screen at least sometimes.

  3. 03 Week 3 · Limits

    Draw a line before the urge shows up.

    Limits work best when you set them in a calm moment, not halfway through a scroll. Use what you learned in week 1 to pick a number that is lower than your current habit, but not so low you will resent it. You can always tighten it next week.

    Set up this week

    1. Limit short-form video, not the whole app. In Reducers, open Short-Form Video Content, turn on the main switch, and choose Time Based or Reel Count Based. The count is combined across apps, so a limit of 20 means 10 Reels plus 10 Shorts and you are done. Messages and search keep working. You can also allow Reels opened from your Instagram DMs.
    2. App Pause for the rest. If a whole app is the problem, give it a Usage Based daily limit, or a Time Based window like evenings only. You can stack one Timed and one Usage group on the same app, for example "only 6 to 9 PM, and only for an hour."
    3. Pick a firmer unlock. Swap the simple wait for something that takes effort, like typing a sentence or stating your intent. Your stated reasons are saved, so you can read them back later and see how many were real.

    One caveat: a single schedule cannot cross midnight. For a late-night block like 11 PM to 2 AM, make two ranges: 11 PM to 12 AM and 12 AM to 2 AM.

  4. 04 Week 4 · Replacement

    Fill the gap with something you actually want.

    Taking scrolling away leaves an empty slot in your day, and an empty slot is where cravings live. This week is about putting something in its place and having a plan for the moments when the urge comes back anyway.

    Set up this week

    1. Short focus sessions. On the Focus tab, create a Focus Group with your scrolling apps, set the slider to 25 minutes and start. Use it for one thing you have been putting off: a book chapter, a walk, a call. Focus Stats keeps a day streak if you like that kind of motivation.
    2. Turn on Urge Surfing. Enable the "I'm struggling" button on your block screens. More on how it works just below.
    3. Review, don't judge. Compare this week's daily and weekly views with week 1. Keep what helped, drop what annoyed you.

Urge surfing, in plain words

A craving feels like it will keep building until you give in. It usually does not. It behaves more like a wave: it rises, peaks and fades, often within a few minutes. Urge surfing means noticing the wave and riding it out instead of trying to fight it or obey it.

In Curbox, tap "I'm struggling" on a block screen. You rate how strong the craving is from 0 to 10, take a moment to reflect, then spend three minutes either playing a calm block puzzle with no score or moving your body with squats, stairs or a fast walk. Then you rate the craving again. Curbox also offers this automatically if you unlock three times in a row, even if the button is turned off.

A "Your cravings" history shows how much your cravings dropped on average. Seeing that number go down, again and again, is good evidence that the urge does pass, which makes the next one easier to wait out.

If you slip

You will have days where you scroll for an hour anyway. That is normal and it does not reset anything. Look at the heatmap, find the hour it happened, and ask what was going on. Tired, bored and avoiding something are the usual suspects. Then adjust one rule for that time of day. A plan that bends is a plan you will still be using in a month.

Questions

Why is doomscrolling so hard to stop?

Endless feeds never give you a natural stopping point, and the next post might always be the good one. That unpredictable reward keeps your thumb moving. Willpower alone has to win every single time, so it helps to change the environment instead: make the habit visible, add a pause, and put limits in place before the urge shows up.

Do I need to delete Instagram or TikTok to stop doomscrolling?

No. Many people only struggle with one part of an app, usually the Reels or Shorts feed. Curbox can limit short-form video by time or by video count while messages, search and the rest of the app keep working. If a whole app is the problem, App Pause can limit or block it instead.

How long does it take to break the scrolling habit?

It varies a lot from person to person, so treat the four weeks here as a pace, not a deadline. The point of going one step per week is that each change has time to feel normal before you add the next one. If a week feels hard, stay on it longer.

What is urge surfing?

Urge surfing is a way of riding out a craving instead of acting on it. A craving tends to rise, peak and fade, often within a few minutes. In Curbox you tap "I'm struggling" on the block screen, rate the craving from 0 to 10, spend three minutes on a calm puzzle or moving your body, then rate it again.

Is Curbox free?

Yes. Curbox is free and open source under the GPL v3, with no ads, no accounts and no subscription. The Android app has no internet permission, so your usage data stays on your phone.
